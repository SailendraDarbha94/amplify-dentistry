## Market map

### Global: a USD 2–3B category held by three ownership blocs

Size the category as a range. 2025 estimates run from USD 1.77B (https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5954780/dental-practice-management-software-market-report) and USD 1.96B (https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/dental-practice-management-software-market-105115) to USD 3.02B (https://straitsresearch.com/report/dental-practice-management-software-market), USD 3.1B (https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/dental-practice-management-software-market) and USD 3.18B (https://www.precedenceresearch.com/dental-practice-management-software-market), with forecast CAGRs of 7.9–10.8% and North America's share anywhere from 38% to 53.6% [corrected]. The 2x spread is scope definition; say "USD 2–3B growing high single digits" and never one number.

Three blocs control most seats, and two are supply distributors that bundle software with consumables and equipment [corrected]:

- Henry Schein One (Henry Schein + Internet Brands JV since 2018) holds Dentrix, Dentrix Ascend, Dentally (UK/IE/AU/NZ/CA), Software of Excellence EXACT, 80% of Jarvis Analytics and 70% of eAssist (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Schein; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210525005482/en/Henry-Schein-One-Acquires-Majority-Interest-in-Jarvis-Analytics). Its 10 March 2026 release claims Dentrix plus Ascend serve 48,000+ US practices and 90% of the top 50 DSOs, ran 191 million eligibility checks in 2025, and now bundles AI voice notes, image QA and a 24/7 support agent into Essentials/Pro/Accelerate tiers (https://investor.henryschein.com/news-releases/news-release-details/2026/Henry-Schein-One-Unveils-the-Next-Era-of-Dentrix-Ascend-for-DSOs-and-Growth-Focused-Practices/default.aspx) [corrected].
- Patterson (Eaglesoft on-prem, Fuse cloud) was taken private by Patient Square Capital at USD 31.35/share, about USD 4.1B, completed 17 April 2025 (https://www.beckersdental.com/supply-chain/patient-square-capital-completes-acquisition-of-patterson-companies/) [corrected].
- Planet DDS (Level Equity and Aquiline) owns Denticon, Apteryx imaging and Cloud 9 ortho, claims 14,500 practices and 175,000 users, and in 2026 pivoted from "cloud-first" to "AI-first" with a DentalOS platform and paid Agents+/Clinical Voice+/Imaging+ suites (https://www.planetdds.com/newsroom/planet-dds-enterprise-dso-adoption/; https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260818000155/en/Planet-DDS-Marks-Mid-Year-Growth-with-Expanded-AI-Agent-Suite-and-New-Platform-Packaging) [disputed on sub-claims; the DSO tilt is confirmed from the release].

Around them: Curve Dental says it is "rapidly approaching" USD 100M ARR, claims "around 20%" of cloud seats with no stated source, announced a USD 200M product investment on 20 August 2026, and asserts 85% of roughly 160,000 North American practices still run legacy client-server software, an unsourced vendor estimate (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/curve-dental-announces-200-million-rd-investment-302856409.html) [corrected]. CareStack, engineered in Trivandrum and sold only in the US, UK and Australia, claims 3,000+ practices and over USD 145M raised, its Indian entity reporting INR 146.9 Cr FY25 revenue (https://carestack.com/; https://inc42.com/company/carestack/) [corrected]. Open Dental publishes USD 199/location/month for 12 months then USD 149, USD 89 in "other countries", and free with limited support in developing countries explicitly including India (https://www.opendental.com/site/fees.html) [confirmed]. Circulating share percentages (Dentrix 18–22%, Eaglesoft 15–20%) trace to SEO listicles with no methodology; cite installed bases, never shares (https://medixdental.com/dental-pms-market-data/) [corrected].

Incumbent strategy is 100+ location DSOs, yet ADA HPI data as read by Medix put DSO-affiliated dentists at 16.1% of US dentists in 2024 (https://medixdental.com/dental-pms-market-data/) [disputed on other sub-claims]. The solo and 2–5 chair practice is the under-served majority even in the US; in India it is the whole market.

### India: four lakh dentists, a USD 90M software market, paper as the incumbent

The National Dental Register lists 405,777 Part-A registered dentists (dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx, read Oct 2026), led by Karnataka (57,125), Maharashtra (55,672), Kerala (37,760), Tamil Nadu (36,824) and Andhra Pradesh (29,000) (https://dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx) [confirmed]; 331 colleges admit 28,114 BDS students a year, roughly 86% of seats private (https://dciindia.gov.in/; https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/dentistry-in-india-oversupply-and-gender-bias-shaping-oral-health-workforce) [corrected]. The DCI was dissolved on 19 March 2026 and the National Dental Commission now runs registration (https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2242888&reg=48&lang=2) [confirmed], so every form and footer must say "NDC / State Dental Council".

Clinic counts are poorly sourced [corrected]. The defensible version: 300,000+ practising dentists, about 95% individual practitioners per a 2011-era estimate (https://www.groupdentistrynow.com/dso-group-blog/exploring-the-evolution-and-future-of-dental-groups-in-india-an-interview-with-dr-aarti-sharma-kapila/), and chains totalling roughly 1,100–1,300 clinics, led by Clove at 715 clinics in May 2026 with FY26 revenue of ~Rs 450 crore, breakeven at ~Rs 4.75 lakh/month and mature clinics at ~Rs 10 lakh/month (https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/deep-dive/clove-dentals-prescription-for-scale/2993977/1), Apollo Dental 220+, Partha 120+ and Sabka Dentist 100 (https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/india-dental-services-market) [corrected]. Ken Research puts organised chains at 6.6% of a USD 4.3bn services market (same source); chains build in-house and are a later upsell, not the ICP.

Software is a USD 88.8M market in 2025, USD 100.1M in 2026 and USD 241.9M by 2033 at 13.4% CAGR, 2.9% of the global total, with web-based deployment at 60.7% share (https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/dental-practice-management-software-market/india) [confirmed].

Willingness to pay is the binding constraint. A Kerala cohort of 400 recent graduates found 27% not working and 54.5% earning INR 15,000/month or less (https://jglobaloralhealth.org/unemployment-and-vulnerable-financial-situation-among-recent-dental-graduates-of-kerala-india-results-from-a-cross-sectional-study/) [corrected; single state, pre-2019]; own-practice dentists net roughly INR 8–40 LPA (https://resumegyani.in/salary/dentist-salary-india). A one-chair tier-2/3 clinic costs INR 15–30 lakh to open, reaches 12–18 patients/day only by month 12, and pays its owner INR 80,000–2 lakh/month in the second half of year one (https://ichelonconsulting.com/insights/dental-clinic-cost-india-2026-complete-breakdown; https://www.dentskart.com/blog/how-to-start-a-dental-practice-in-india-2026) [corrected; vendor estimates]. Receptionists cost INR 12,000–22,000/month and setup guides say to go without one for 3–4 months (https://www.dentskart.com/blog/how-to-start-a-dental-practice-in-india-2026) [confirmed], so the dentist is biller, scheduler and WhatsApp operator. Software at INR 1,000–2,000/month is 1–2% of a year-one owner's take-home and under 0.5% of a mature Clove clinic's revenue.

Records are still paper. A 344-respondent 2023 survey found intraoral findings on paper only for 51% versus 34% hybrid, a chairside computer for 49%, and fear of data loss (47.5%) plus assistants' reluctance to type (35.5%) as top barriers (https://www.ovid.com/jnls/srmj/fulltext/10.4103/srmjrds.srmjrds_144_23~evaluating-the-knowledge-and-perception-of-electronic-health) [corrected]; a 2025 Bengaluru survey of 138 dentists found 91.3% digital radiography but only 34.8% EHR use (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44445-026-00204-5). WhatsApp is the clinical channel: a Maharashtra survey of 265 dentists (PMC12375830, published 2025, fieldwork 2023) found 88.3% preferred WhatsApp for patient data and only 46.0% always obtained consent (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12375830/) [confirmed]. Meta's India rate is INR 0.115 per utility message and INR 0.8631 per marketing message plus 18% GST (https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/) [confirmed].

## Why dental-only wins

### The record is spatial, not narrative

A dental chart is organised around a tooth, its five surfaces and six periodontal sites, not an encounter note. The mature model, documented in Open Dental's public manual and schema, attaches every procedure to a treatment area (tooth, surface, quadrant, sextant, arch, mouth), a tooth number in one of four nomenclatures, a surface string with a Class V flag that must be stripped on claims, and a status lifecycle (TP, Complete, Existing, Referred, Condition, TPi) in which "a procedure can NEVER be deleted"; perio is a separate 6-site by 32-tooth matrix with gingival margin, auto-CAL, furcation, mobility and bleeding flags across up to six overlaid exams (https://opendental.com/Manual/entertreatment.html; https://www.opendental.com/manual/perio.html; https://www.opendental.com/OpenDentalDocumentation24-1.xml) [corrected]. Generic EHRs have none of it: OpenEMR's 35 form types include no dental form, GNU Health documents no dentistry module, and openEHR's only tooth archetype is a v0 draft with free-text tooth labels (https://api.github.com/repos/openemr/openemr/contents/interface/forms; https://docs.gnuhealth.org/his/userguide/modules/index.html; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openEHR/CKM-mirror/master/local/archetypes/cluster/openEHR-EHR-CLUSTER.exam-tooth.v0.adl) [confirmed].

The strongest Indian evidence is Clove Dental's CIO, who evaluated 10+ EMRs, found none "truly dental", and built an in-house web EMR-cum-CRM designed point-of-sale style with large touch buttons and minimal typing, plus a "Clove Doctor" app for images and X-rays (https://www.ciol.com/cxo-of-the-week-vikas-sood-cio-clove-dental/) [confirmed]. The best-funded operator in the country validated both halves of the thesis: generic fails, and the winning interaction model is tap-heavy and image-first.

### The ancillary workflows are dental, and generic software never reaches them

Lab cases need per-service turnaround that computes a due date skipping holidays, shade, tooth numbers and Created/Sent/Received/QC timestamps (https://www.opendental.com/manual243/labcaseedit.html; https://carestack.com/dental-software/features/lab-case-management) [confirmed]. Implants need UDI, lot and serial captured chairside; EU MDR Article 18 implant cards have applied to dental implants since 26 May 2021 (https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2021/01/CED-DOC-2020-021-FIN-E.pdf) [confirmed]. Medical history needs structured rules: SDCEP requires the assigned MRONJ risk level to be recorded in the clinical record and keys DOAC handling to the procedure's bleeding-risk category, not a free-text allergies box (https://www.sdcep.org.uk/published-guidance/medication-related-osteonecrosis-of-the-jaw/; https://www.sdcep.org.uk/published-guidance/anticoagulants-and-antiplatelets/) [disputed on incidence figures; structure confirmed]. Imaging needs tooth-linked radiographs because DICOM still has "no method currently to denote precisely which teeth are present in a given intra-oral radiograph" (https://wiki.ihe.net/index.php/Display_Mapping_and_Viewing_Solution_for_Intraoral_Radiography) [corrected]. Each is a module a generic clinic EMR will never prioritise and a dental-only product can own.

### Billing and regulation diverge from medicine, and by country

US incumbents are architected around the insurance revenue cycle (Ascend's 191 million eligibility checks and ~100 million claims a year) (https://investor.henryschein.com/news-releases/news-release-details/2026/Henry-Schein-One-Unveils-the-Next-Era-of-Dentrix-Ascend-for-DSOs-and-Growth-Focused-Practices/default.aspx) [corrected]. In India dental cover is an OPD sub-limit of Rs 1,500–25,000 with waiting periods (Tata AIG: two years) (https://www.tataaig.com/health-insurance/dental-insurance; https://joinditto.in/articles/health-insurance/dental-health-insurance/) [corrected], so that core is dead weight and the money features are UPI, EMI, staged payments and family ledgers. Indian GST is dental-specific: health-care services are exempt under Notification 12/2017 entry 74 (SAC 999312), a 2022 Maharashtra AAR taxed bleaching and smile-design veneers under SAC 999722, and the 22 September 2025 move of Group 99972 to 5% without ITC leaves cosmetic dentistry at either 5% or 18% pending a CBIC clarification (https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/no-gst-on-providing-dental-health-care-services-by-dental-clinic-aar/; https://www.gstindiaonline.com/pages/gstlegislation/GST-Nfy/Central%20Tax%20Rate/2025/15-2025-CTR.pdf) [disputed; make the rate configurable]. The UK differs again: NHS England charges from 1 April 2026 are £27.90, £76.60 and £332.10 per course of treatment, the highest band setting the charge (https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-02003/en-us) [corrected]. Billing must be a pluggable strategy, not a schema.

The DCI Code of Ethics 2014, still operative under the NDC Act's transitional clause, requires records kept at least three years from start of treatment, copies within 72 hours, the state registration number on every prescription, certificate and receipt, and a cost estimate before treatment (https://dciindia.gov.in/Rule_Regulation/Gazette_Notification_reg_DCI_Revised_Dentists_Code_of_Ethics_Regulations_2014_27.06.2014.pdf) [confirmed]. Consumer fora, read on Indian Kanoon in a single unverified pass, awarded Rs 5 lakh where an RCT had no written consent and the patient was not told the tooth would become non-vital, about Rs 6.3 lakh against Sabka Dentist in August 2026 for nine RCTs without pre-operative IOPAs, and Rs 3 lakh where a minor's orthodontic extraction lacked the father's written consent (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/29669611/; https://indiankanoon.org/doc/82459637/; https://indiankanoon.org/doc/85276897/) [gap-fill-unverified]; "not maintaining proper treatment record" was itself held to be negligence. These are dental defensibility rules a generic EMR cannot encode.

### The market rewards clinical opinion

Oryx, dentist-founded, charges USD 650–1,399 per location and was Inc. 5000's #1 dental PMS in 2025 (https://www.oryxdental.com/pricing/; https://www.oryxdental.com/about/) [corrected]; Cloud 9's orthodontic depth was bought by Planet DDS in January 2023 and Dentally's NHS-integrated depth by Henry Schein One in July 2020 (https://www.planetdds.com/newsroom/planet-dds-announces-its-acquisition-of-cloud-9/; https://blog.softwareofexcellence.com/en-gb/software-of-excellence-acquires-dentally) [corrected]. Specialty depth is both the moat and the exit thesis: generic vendors cannot cheaply replicate it, so they buy it.

## Unfair advantages

### 1. The dentist's phone is the clinical surface

No major vendor markets phone-first charting: ODTouch is tablet-only and US-only at USD 85/month with its Chart screen "not available when using a mobile phone device", Curve Mobile lists schedule, texting and MMS photos with no charting, Dentrix Ascend's app is for VoIP calls and Dentally runs "just using Google Chrome" (https://www.opendental.com/manual/odtouchchart.html; https://www.curvedental.com/mobile; https://www.dentrixascend.com/dental-solutions/marketing-and-patient-experience/transform-calls-into-care/; https://www.henryscheinone.com/products/dentally/) [corrected]. In India, Practo Ray cannot record surfaces and Dentee's iOS app sits at 1.7/5 (https://yourdentalreview.com/practo-patient-management-software-ray/; https://apps.apple.com/in/app/dentee-for-doctors/id1081805174). pub.dev has no odontogram package, only whole-tooth selectors with 7–10 likes (https://pub.dev/packages?q=odontogram; https://pub.dev/packages/dental_teeth_selector) [corrected]. The FDI-keyed, surface-level, mixed-dentition CustomPainter odontogram with a six-site perio grid is the first thing to build and the thing nobody can copy from a package. Decision (recommended): open-source only the tooth geometry and FDI/Universal/Palmer notation layer as a small `tooth_geometry` package; keep surfaces, conditions, perio, history and sync in the proprietary `chart_engine`, because the surface-level odontogram is the one thing competitors cannot pull from a package.

### 2. Offline-first with cloud sync

Only KiviHealth and (contestedly) Clinicea claim offline mode among Indian clinic tools; BestoSys, Practo Ray and DentSoft are online-only (https://kivihealth.com/pricing; https://www.dentsoft.in/) [corrected]. Demand is visible: 76.3% of 384 North Indian dental professionals named network access as the top barrier to teledentistry (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12451562/) [corrected]. Cloud incumbents are fragile: a Curve user reported being down "six plus hours" (relayed by RevUp, undated) and the Change Healthcare attack of 21 February 2024 stopped US e-claims for weeks (https://revupdental.com/best-dental-practice-management-systems/; https://www.ameritas.com/newsroom/update-dental-and-vision-claim-processing/) [corrected]. Drift plus PowerSync plus Supabase in Mumbai is the stack; "works when the Jio tower doesn't" is the pitch, and web-first vendors cannot retrofit it quickly.

### 3. WhatsApp as the default channel, with consent logged

88.3% of Maharashtra dentists prefer WhatsApp for patient data and only 46% always obtain consent (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12375830/) [confirmed]. Meta charges INR 0.115 per utility message (https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/) [confirmed], yet Jaldee sells WhatsApp as a INR 2,999/yr add-on, BestoSys as an optional add-on, Fresha retails it at INR 1.60–5.50 per message, and Practo Ray, Dentee and HealthPlix do not list it (https://jaldeehealth.com/pricing/; https://www.bestosys.com/plans; https://www.fresha.com/pricing) [corrected]. Bundle a fair-use utility quota (1,500 messages a month in Clinic, about 500 visits at three touches; 5,000 in Plus; overage at Meta cost plus GST), meter marketing at INR 0.86, and attach a one-tap consent record to every photo or radiograph shared for a second opinion or lab. One caveat binds the architecture: WhatsApp's policy bars health information where regulations require heightened protection, so send deep links to an authenticated portal for consents, plans and images rather than the clinical content itself (https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) [corrected].

### 4. Published flat per-clinic pricing, no add-on ladder, no payment percentage

Price creep after signing is the most common complaint across Denticon (fixed annual % increases), Curve, NexHealth and tab32, whose Alpine plan rises from USD 125 to 225/month in year two plus USD 0.20 per claim and USD 1.25 per eligibility check (https://revupdental.com/best-dental-practice-management-systems/; https://tab32.com/pricing/) [corrected]. In India, Practo Ray takes 2% (Atom) or 1.8% (Clinic Management) on collections and Clinicea prices per practitioner (https://www.practo.com/providers/clinics/ray/plans; https://clinicea.com/pricing) [confirmed]. Cliniko, bootstrapped, has not changed its flat USD 45–395 bands since June 2011 (https://www.cliniko.com/pricing/) [confirmed]. Ship a public page: free solo tier, INR 1,499/clinic/month with up to three doctors, INR 2,999 multi-doctor, the line "no 2% on your collections", and a contractual one-tap CSV/PDF/DICOM export, since Eaglesoft's proprietary imaging is the documented switching barrier in the US (https://revupdental.com/best-dental-practice-management-systems/) [corrected] and Open Dental's still-open MySQL database is the easiest migration source (https://www.opendental.com/site/license.html) [confirmed]. Gateway fees (Razorpay's 2% plus GST even on zero-MDR UPI) are a pass-through you cannot abolish, only decline to mark up; publish one published payments rule: ₹0 on cash and static-QR UPI collected in clinic; a single printed 0.25% platform split, over the payment aggregator's negotiated rate, only on gateway-routed remote payments and EMI; UPI MDR passed through as its own line (https://razorpay.com/pricing/) [disputed on details].

### 5. Cash-first billing with a GST class on every line

Pre-tag every procedure EXEMPT (SAC 999312), COSMETIC (5% without ITC or 18%, configurable per the clinic's CA) or GOODS (HSN 9021 at 5%), issue a Rule 46A invoice-cum-bill of supply to unregistered patients, and keep GSTIN optional because an exempt-only clinic need not register at all (https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/no-gst-on-providing-dental-health-care-services-by-dental-clinic-aar/; https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/faq.pdf) [disputed on the cosmetic rate; structure settled]. Collect via UPI Intent on Android and dynamic QR on desktop web, since UPI Collect was deprecated on 28 February 2026 (https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/upi/) [disputed on minor details]. Model the 0.4% UPI MDR above Rs 2,000 (capped at Rs 300) scheduled for 15 October 2026 as a pass-through line behind a feature flag, because deferral to January 2027 is being sought (https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-above-rs-2000-october-15/) [gap-fill-unverified]. Insurance becomes a "reimbursement pack" export, not a claims engine.

### 6. Procedure-specific e-consent with tooth numbers, DPDP-ready

Your RCT, extraction, crown and RPD/FPD/CD templates with {tooth_numbers} placeholders are a genuine head start: Open Dental treats procedure-specific consent with tooth-number input areas as a first-class feature, but neither Practo Ray nor Dentee advertises tooth-numbered e-consent (https://www.opendental.com/manual243/consentforms.html; https://www.practo.com/providers/clinics/ray) [corrected]. Make the treatment plan itself the e-sign object (line items with FDI numbers, price, consent text), capture OTP-verified identity, timestamp, template hash and signer relationship (guardian for under-18s), add a dentist's "what I explained" note because fora look for the conversation, and offer Aadhaar eSign for implants and surgery. Build the separate purpose-scoped data-processing consent now: DPDP's notice, consent and children's-data obligations commence 13 May 2027 with penalties up to Rs 250 crore (https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/0ecfe5fee4f08c90ee7a1dbb5b2e6b7c.pdf; https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf) [corrected].

### 7. Multi-sitting plans, lab job cards and implant lots

RCT plus crown (INR 8,000–25,000+) and single implants (INR 25,000–80,000) are where clinic revenue lives (https://dentalarchindia.com/root-canal-treatment-cost-india/; https://www.fmsdental.com/dental-implant-cost/) [corrected], and they are multi-visit and lab-dependent. Outsourced lab-case tracking is not addressed by Practo Ray or Clinicea, whose INR 499/technician add-on is for in-house labs, and is covered only by small dental-only vendors (https://www.clinicea.com/pricing; https://dento365.in/) [corrected]. Model stages (RCT 1/2/3; prep, try-in, cement), lab slips to WhatsApp with shade and FDI numbers, remake tracking, and barcode capture of implant UDI, lot and expiry with a patient implant passport PDF (https://medicaldevicehq.com/articles/mdr-article-18-implant-card-and-information/) [confirmed].

### 8. A procedure-driven recall engine that prices the overdue list

Copy Open Dental's pattern where each completed prophylaxis or perio maintenance auto-creates the next due date (https://www.opendental.com/OpenDentalDocumentation24-1.xml) [unverified-low-risk], with NICE CG19's 3–24 month interval menu (https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/CG19/chapter/recommendations) [confirmed]. Evidence that it pays is directional, not Indian: Sesame's 1.6M-appointment vendor study cut no-shows 22.95% with no control group (https://us.dental-tribune.com/news/study-reveals-how-automated-patient-appointment-reminders-affect-dental-practice-no-show-rates-and-production/) [corrected], and a 29-study non-dental review found reminders cut non-attendance about 34% relative (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=PMCID:PMC3188816&resultType=core&format=json) [corrected]. Show the rupee value of the overdue list on the home screen, instrument your own funnel, and publish the first real Indian benchmark.

### 9. Inspection-ready compliance logs

No generic clinic app prints sterilisation, biomedical-waste, AERB and licence records, yet CPCB issued 14,783 show-cause notices to defaulting health-care facilities in 2024, up from 8,082, and is forwarding lists of never-authorised small clinics to state health departments (https://cpcb.gov.in/uploads/Projects/Bio-Medical-Waste/AR_BMWM_2024.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified; read from the primary PDF]. AERB counts 30,557 licensed dental X-ray units, requires QA at least every two years, and found about 90% of >10 mSv badge readings were "non-genuine" from badge misuse (https://www.aerb.gov.in/images/PDF/Annual_report/ar2024/English/Annual_report-2024-eng.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified]. NABH DHSP Section B (1–15 chairs, 80 standards, 416 objective elements) specifies the logs: per-load sterilisation numbering, monthly facility rounds, a consent-required procedure list, displayed tariffs (https://nabh-portal-live.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/13105241/Final-Dental-Standard-3rd-Edition.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified]. A daily BMW register, weekly spore-test reminder, QA due-date timer and licence wallet is cheap, sticky and sellable to the owner.

### 10. The first ABDM-certified dental-only app, FHIR from day one

HPR lists "Dentistry" with eleven dental specialities verified by State Dental Councils, HFR offers "Dental Clinic" with a dental-chairs field and no Clinical Establishments Act prerequisite, and the National Dental Register already runs at ndr.abdm.gov.in (https://nhpr.abdm.gov.in/nhpr/v4/assets/index-7a48ab1a.js; https://ndr.abdm.gov.in/) [gap-fill-unverified; read from the portal bundle]. NRCeS's FHIR IG v6.5.0 has no dental profile (https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html) [confirmed], so encode tooth findings as SNOMED-coded Condition and Procedure entries with tooth bodySite inside OPConsultRecord, and adopt SNOMED International's July-2026 Odontogram refset, free under the India affiliate licence (https://mlds.ihtsdotools.org/api/releasePackages/173263; https://www.nrces.in/faqs) [gap-fill-unverified]. DHIS pays only Rs 5 per record above 100 a month (https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/DHIS_corrigendum_7_c1d7e09cc2.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified], so the value is the mandate trajectory: NMC already names non-compliant private colleges and NDC sits on the same infrastructure (https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/951). Budget M1/M2 plus HFR/HPR in the first 90 days, certification in days 90–150, M3 by month six.

### 11. Assistant-friendly, Indian-language, voice-first capture

35.5% of surveyed dentists cite assistants' reluctance to type and 47.5% fear data loss (https://www.ovid.com/jnls/srmj/fulltext/10.4103/srmjrds.srmjrds_144_23~evaluating-the-knowledge-and-perception-of-electronic-health) [corrected]; 92.4% of North Indian dental professionals have no digital training (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12451562/) [confirmed]. Western vendors meter AI notes (Dentally 45/90 transcriptions per surgery per month; Oryx's AI tier at USD 1,399) (https://www.dentally.com/pricing; https://www.oryxdental.com/pricing/) [corrected] and none offers Hindi or Telugu. A tooth-numbered voice grammar ("16 MB 3-2-4 bleeding"), procedure note templates, generic-name-in-capitals prescriptions with Schedule H/H1 tags and Telugu/Hindi consent text are unregulated to build; Bola, Videa and Kiroku ship ambient scribing without clearance (https://bola.ai/; https://www.trykiroku.com/) [confirmed].

### 12. Partner hooks for AI imaging instead of building it

Expose an imaging API so Pearl (40+ native PMS integrations) and Overjet are partners rather than rebuilds: each AI pathology read is a separate FDA 510(k) and, in India, a CDSCO Class B/C device licence (https://www.hellopearl.com/products/second-opinion; https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/) [corrected], while Oryx already sells partner AI reads inside its USD 1,399 tier (https://www.oryxdental.com/pricing/) [corrected].

## Risks

### Market

- Willingness to pay is INR 500–2,000/clinic/month and the floor is crowded: Dontin free/999, Adrine 999, Dento365 999, DentsKart 999, Healcon 499, Open Dental free (https://www.adrine.in/blog/best-dental-practice-management-software-india; https://www.techjockey.com/category/dental-software; https://dento365.in/; https://www.dentskart.com/). Price is not a moat.
- The real competitor is paper plus a personal WhatsApp number; if the app is slower than a pen in week one it is abandoned. Dento365's "68% on paper" is an uncited marketing tile (https://dento365.in/) [corrected] and no independent adoption survey exists, so run 15–20 founder interviews and a 100-dentist IDA poll before fixing price.
- Practo owns discovery with 10,000+ clinics on Ray and 1 lakh+ doctor partners (https://www.practo.com/providers/clinics) [confirmed]; reconcile its bookings, do not fight it for patients.
- A funded entrant is signalling India: DentalPin (BSL 1.1, Dentaltix-backed, 86 stars) already ships india_gst, razorpay and WhatsApp modules, and its licence forbids forking it into a competing SaaS (https://github.com/dentalpin/dentalpin) [corrected]; NHA is also pushing a free government "eSushrut Clinic" HMIS (https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/1808) [gap-fill-unverified].
- TAM is roughly USD 90–100M a year today; 10,000 clinics at a ₹1,500 blended ARPU is about ₹18 crore ARR (₹12 crore at ₹1,000), so distribution cost per clinic must be near zero (self-serve, referral, WhatsApp onboarding, the AmpDent student funnel).

### Regulatory

- NDC's Ethics and Dental Registration Board has issued no conduct, records, advertising or teledentistry regulation; the 2014 DCI Code continues under s.59(2) (https://dciindia.gov.in/GazetteList.aspx) [gap-fill-unverified]; keep regulator names and retention rules data-driven.
- Teledentistry has no safe harbour: the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines bind only IMC-registered RMPs (https://nmcn.in/public/assets/pdf/Telemedicine%20Practice%20Guidelines.pdf) [confirmed]. Label the feature tele-triage/follow-up, block Schedule X/NDPS, never market "TPG-compliant".
- DPDP's substantive duties commence 13 May 2027, with a MeitY proposal to compress to November 2026 unconfirmed (https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/) [corrected]; CERT-In's 6-hour incident reporting and 180-day log retention already apply (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) [corrected].
- GST on cosmetic dentistry is unsettled (5% without ITC versus 18%) and itemised implants and crowns need a composite-supply opinion; UPI MDR from 15 October 2026 may be deferred or stayed, with Supreme Court notices issued on 29 September 2026 (https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-above-rs-2000-october-15/) [gap-fill-unverified].
- Never hold patient funds: RBI's PA Master Direction requires Rs 15 crore net worth and merchant funds credited only to the merchant's own bank account (https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/141MD7D7F25DEBF1F48449E20D685E4B014E5.PDF) [gap-fill-unverified]; use sub-merchant splits (Razorpay Route needs Rs 40 lakh turnover or a self-declaration) and get an opinion on s.194-O/s.52 e-commerce-operator exposure.
- Any "AI detects caries" copy makes the app a medical device under CDSCO's 2026 software guidance and the FDA's January 2026 CDS guidance (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/; https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software) [corrected; confirmed].
- Google Play requires health apps to publish from an Organization account with a D-U-N-S number; Apple bars PHI in iCloud and requires in-app account deletion (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en; https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) [disputed on effective dates; requirements confirmed].
- US expansion needs an ADA CDT commercial licence with unpublished royalties and 2–4 weeks' processing (https://www.ada.org/publications/ada-store-products/licensing-for-commercial-users) [confirmed]; SNOMED needs an international affiliate licence the moment a non-India user exists (https://www.nrces.in/faqs) [confirmed].

### Technical

- The odontogram, perio grid and image annotation must be hand-built as CustomPainter; no pub.dev package has surfaces, conditions or perio (https://pub.dev/packages/dental_teeth_selector) [disputed on package counts; conclusion firm], and performance on the low-end Android phones common in Indian clinics is unverified.
- Flutter web's wasm renderer runs only on Chromium 119+ (https://docs.flutter.dev/platform-integration/web/wasm) [confirmed]; spike the front-desk web surface before choosing Flutter web over Next.js.
- Isar is stalled, Realm/Atlas Device Sync reached end-of-life on 30 September 2025 and ElectricSQL has no official Dart client; Drift 2.35 plus PowerSync is the live path, and PowerSync's free tier (50 peak clients) will be exceeded at launch, so budget Pro at USD 49/month (https://github.com/isar/isar; https://powersync.com/pricing) [corrected; confirmed].
- ABDM certification needs three WASA "Safe-to-Host" certificates (web, Android, iOS) renewed at least annually plus NHA functional testing capped at 7 working days; the year-one compliance reserve is roughly INR 3–10 lakh on vendor quotes (https://sandboxcms.abdm.gov.in/api/faqs?populate=*; https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/Comprehensive_Cyber_Security_Audit_Policy_Guidelines.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified].
- Imaging: the only pure-Dart DICOM viewer throws on JPEG 2000 and JPEG-LS (https://pub.dev/packages/dicom_viewer) [confirmed], and intraoral sensors capture through Windows TWAIN drivers (https://globalimagingusa.com/blogs/blog/choosing-the-right-intraoral-sensor) [corrected], so a Windows capture bridge is unavoidable for RVG; make the phone camera the imaging device first.
- India-resident hosting is a practical must for ABDM (callback server in India, NHA NAT IPs whitelisted) (https://sandboxcms.abdm.gov.in/uploads/FAQ_20_11_2025_808a25df64.pdf) [gap-fill-unverified]; Supabase has Mumbai but no Gulf region (https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions) [confirmed].

### Founder capacity

- One founder, new to Flutter, with Next.js, NestJS and Expo muscle memory: the heavy-canvas requirement argues for Flutter, but the hiring pool is thinner (a mid-2025 US LinkedIn snapshot showed about 6,413 React Native against 1,068 Flutter postings; no Indian split verified) (https://devnewsletter.com/p/state-of-flutter-2026/) [disputed].
- The scope above is at least eight modules; the MVP must be chart, bill, WhatsApp, recall and lab with 3–5 dashboard numbers, not 40 reports. The "31 features, I use 4" line is an anonymous vendor-blog quote, but the warning stands (https://codingclave.com/guides/best-dental-clinic-software-india-2026) [corrected]; same-day Telugu/Hindi WhatsApp support is also a labour cost one person cannot carry past a few hundred clinics.
- Naming: ampdent.com belongs to a California events company, "AMP – Accelerate My Practice" is a US dental coaching brand, amplifydentistry.com no longer resolves, and no IP-India class 9/42/44 search has been done (https://ampdent.com/company/; https://acceleratemypractice.com/amp/) [corrected]; file Indian marks before launch and consider a distinct clinic-app name.
- Cash: Supabase Pro plus PowerSync Pro plus Codemagic's free tier is under USD 100/month at 100 clinics, but ABDM certification, WASA renewals, SNOMED sub-licence reporting, DLT SMS registration (INR 5,900) and CA/legal opinions on GST, PA status and ECO exposure must be funded before revenue (https://supabase.com/pricing; https://www.messagecentral.com/blog/a-complete-guide-on-dlt-registration) [confirmed; corrected].
- No survey quantifies Indian clinics' paper-versus-software use, no-show rates or ticket sizes, and the forum evidence here is secondhand; instrument Oasis Dental Clinic as baseline zero and do a browser pass on dentist forums before writing marketing copy.
