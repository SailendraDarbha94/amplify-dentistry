## Summary

India's regulatory picture for a dental-only clinic app changed materially in the last 12 months and is now reasonably legible. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were gazetted on 13/14 Nov 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) with a phased rollout: the Data Protection Board provisions are already live, the Consent Manager regime (Rule 4) switches on ~13 Nov 2026, and every substantive data-fiduciary duty (Rules 3, 5-16, 22: itemised notice, consent, Rule 6 security safeguards, Rule 7 breach notification with a 72-hour detailed report to the Board, Rule 8 erasure with 48-hour pre-erasure notice and 1-year log retention, Rule 14 90-day grievance SLA) applies from ~13 May 2027, with MeitY publicly floating a compression to Nov 2026. Health data is NOT a separate "sensitive" class under DPDP (DISHA was never enacted; the SPDI Rules 2011 fade out when Section 43A is repealed), but penalties are large (Rs 250 cr security / Rs 200 cr breach-notice / Rs 200 cr children's data) and CERT-In's separate 6-hour incident-reporting direction (28 Apr 2022) plus 180-day in-India log retention already apply to any "body corporate". The professional layer flipped too: the National Dental Commission replaced the DCI on 19 Mar 2026 and repealed the Dentists Act 1948, but the DCI Revised Code of Ethics 2014 is the only operative record-keeping rule for dentists (3-year minimum retention from start of treatment, records handed over within 72 hours, registration number on every prescription/receipt, fee estimates before treatment, factual websites with fees allowed, no self-photos on signboards, no claims of superiority). Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 formally bind only IMC-registered RMPs, so there is no binding teledentistry rule yet; build to TPG 2020 as best practice. ABDM is clearly in scope for dentists: state dental councils (AP mandatory; Kerala, Haryana, Rajasthan) are pushing HPR IDs, dental clinics register on HFR, and a vendor certifies once (any two of M1/M2/M3, NHA-empanelled functional test, CERT-In-empanelled WASA "Safe-to-Host") and then maps each clinic's HFR ID. The ABDM Health Data Management Policy requires India-resident storage, which is the practical reason to host in India from day one even though DPDP itself only uses a negative list. Standards are free-ish: SNOMED CT is free in India under an NRCeS affiliate licence (an international licence is needed the moment a non-India user exists), LOINC is free, FHIR R4 profiles are published by NRCeS (IG v6.5.0, May 2025: OPConsultRecord, PrescriptionRecord, DiagnosticReportRecord, InvoiceRecord etc.; nothing dental-specific). GST: dental healthcare services are exempt (SAC 9993, Notification 12/2017; Maharashtra AAR Jyoti Ceramic 2022 confirmed crowns/bridges/restoratives exempt) but teeth whitening/veneers for smile design (SAC 999722) are taxable and goods such as implants sold to outpatients are taxable, so invoices must split exempt vs taxable lines. RBI's card-on-file rules (1 Jan 2022) forbid any non-issuer storing PAN, so the app must never touch raw card data (gateway tokens only, PCI SAQ A posture).

Globally the same architecture carries, with stricter numbers. HIPAA (US, dentists are covered entities): BAAs with every cloud/SaaS vendor, audit controls, 60-day breach notice, 6-year documentation retention, record retention set by state law (ADA guidance: ~7 years adults, until 7 years after majority for minors); the Jan 2025 Security Rule NPRM (mandatory encryption, MFA, asset inventory/network map, 72-hour restore, 6-monthly vulnerability scans, annual pen test, annual BA verification) is still proposed, now slated for ~July 2027, but is the right target spec. UK/EU: dental records are Article 9 special-category data processed under Art 9(2)(h) health-care (not consent), a DPIA is mandatory for large-scale health processing, UK dental records are retained 11 years (children to age 25/11 years whichever longer), CQC registration and GDC standards apply, and NHS-contract practices submit the DSP Toolkit. Australia: NDB scheme applies to every health service provider regardless of turnover (30-day assessment ceiling), state law retains records 7 years/children to 25, APP 8 requires telling patients where offshore data lives. UAE Federal Law 2/2019 Article 13 keeps health data in-country (AED 500k-2M fines) and Saudi PDPL + NPHIES + ZATCA e-invoicing make in-Kingdom hosting and claims integration table stakes. App stores: Google Play's Health Content & Services policy (effective 28 Aug 2025) requires every developer to file the Health apps declaration (category "Healthcare services and management"), a public non-PDF privacy policy, a "not a medical device" disclaimer, and bans Play Billing for regulated clinical services; Apple 1.4.1/5.1.3 forbids storing PHI in iCloud and 5.1.1(v) requires in-app account deletion.

Day-one data-model mandates that fall out of this: (1) immutable, append-only audit log per record with date/time, record id, user id, action and reason (ISO 27789 per EHR Standards 2016; HIPAA audit controls; DPDP Rule 6), with clinical entries versioned, never hard-deleted (strike-through semantics, ADA/EHR-Standards); (2) a first-class Consent table separate from treatment consent: purpose-scoped DPDP consents (treatment, billing, insurance, marketing, research, third-party sharing), each with notice version, language, timestamp, channel, withdrawal timestamp, plus ABDM consent artefacts and procedure consent forms with e-signature; (3) guardian/nominee relationships: verified parent/guardian for under-18s (DPDP s.9; dentistry is heavily paediatric), DPDP s.14 nominee, and age-of-majority-based retention; (4) tenant-level data-residency and region pinning (India region for all Indian tenants; UK/EU/AU/UAE/KSA regions later) and per-tenant retention policies with legal-hold flags and scheduled erasure with 48-hour pre-notice; (5) export (patient record within 72 hours per DCI 3.3.2; HIPAA right of access 30 days; GDPR 1 month) and erasure workflows that respect statutory retention; (6) breach register with multi-clock timers (CERT-In 6h, DPDP 72h, HIPAA 60d, OAIC 30d, ABDM NHA channel); (7) prescriptions carrying dentist name, degrees, state registration number, HPR ID, generic names, and dispensed-by-clinic flag; (8) invoices with SAC/HSN codes and exempt/taxable split; (9) identifiers: ABHA number/address, HPR ID, HFR ID, state council reg no., NDC national register number; (10) terminology bindings: SNOMED CT (with ISO 3950/FDI tooth numbering), ICD-10 via SNOMED map, LOINC, and FHIR R4 export per NRCeS profiles.

### 1. `corrected` · confidence high

### 2. `corrected` · confidence high

The DPDP Act, 2023 (Act 22 of 2023) has no separate "sensitive personal data"/health-data category: the word "sensitive" does not appear in the enacted text (unlike the SPDI Rules 2011 under IT Act s.43A, and the never-enacted 2018 MoHFW DISHA draft, which was shelved in favour of DPDP). Any clinic that decides the purpose and means of processing patients' digital personal data is a "Data Fiduciary" (s.2(i)). Processing needs either consent under s.6 (free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, limited to the specified purpose; each purpose such as treatment/records, billing, insurance claims, research, marketing or third-party sharing must be separately specified) or a "legitimate use" under s.7. The medical-emergency exemption is s.7(f) (threat to life or immediate threat to health), epidemics/public-health is s.7(g) and disasters s.7(h) — the Act's s.7 has lettered clauses (a)-(i) with no sub-section (1), so "7(1)(d)" is a wrong citation. Clinical/procedure consent is a different legal act and is widely read (e.g. by DPDP compliance commentary) as not substituting for s.6 data-processing consent, though the Act does not say this expressly. Notice requirements: Rule 3 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), notified 13 Nov 2025) requires the notice to be standalone, in clear and plain language, with (i) an itemised description of the personal data and (ii) the specified purpose(s) and description of goods/services, plus a communication link to the fiduciary's website/app (and other means) for withdrawing consent, exercising rights and complaining to the Data Protection Board. The language requirement is NOT in Rule 3 but in s.5(3) of the Act: the fiduciary must give the option to access the notice in English or any of the (22) languages in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution. Critically for timing: as of October 2026 these obligations are not yet in force — under Rule 1(4), Rule 3 and Rules 5-16, 22-23 commence 18 months after publication (i.e. ~13/14 May 2027; Rule 4 on Consent Managers from ~13/14 Nov 2026; only Rules 1, 2, 17-21 took effect on 13 Nov 2025). A MeitY proposal to shorten the 18-month window to 12 months has been reported but not notified. Until the relevant Act sections are commenced (s.44(2)(a) omits IT Act s.43A), the SPDI Rules 2011 remain the operative health-data regime for Indian clinics.

**Why it matters:** Design two distinct consent objects: clinical procedure consent (your existing RCT/extraction/crown templates) and purpose-scoped data-processing consent with notice version, language and withdrawal history. Multi-language notice text (Telugu, Hindi, etc.) is a product feature, not localisation polish.

### 3. `corrected` · confidence high

Breach obligations in India stack on up to three (not confirmed four) regimes, two of which are verified against primary text. (1) DPDP Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025), Rule 7: on becoming aware of a personal data breach the Data Fiduciary must (a) intimate each affected Data Principal "without delay" with a description of the breach (nature, extent, timing), likely consequences, mitigation measures, safety steps she may take, and business contact of a responder; and (b) intimate the Data Protection Board "without delay" with a description (nature, extent, timing, location, likely impact), then within 72 hours of becoming aware (or longer if the Board allows on written request) provide updated detailed information, broad facts/circumstances/reasons, mitigation measures, findings on the person who caused the breach, remedial measures, and the report of intimations to Data Principals. There is NO prescribed form: "Form DPB-1" does not appear anywhere in the gazetted Rules. Critically, as of 10 Oct 2026 Rule 7 is NOT yet in force — Rule 1(4) brings Rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23 into force 18 months after publication, i.e. 13 May 2027 (only Rules 1, 2, 17-21 were effective immediately; Rule 4 on 13 Nov 2026). (2) CERT-In Directions under s.70B IT Act dated 28 Apr 2022 (effective ~27 Jun 2022, 60 days after issue): report Annexure-I incidents — which do list "Data Breach" (xi), "Data Leak" (xii) and malicious code incl. Ransomware (v) — to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing (partial info first, remainder "within reasonable time" per FAQ Q30); enable and keep logs of all ICT systems for a rolling 180 days "within the Indian jurisdiction" (CERT-In FAQ Q35 softens this: logs may be stored outside India provided they can be produced to CERT-In in reasonable time); sync clocks to NIC/NPL NTP servers (entities spanning multiple geographies may use another accurate source that does not deviate from NPL/NIC). (3) ABDM/NHA incident reporting for integrated HIPs: asserted only by a secondary compliance vendor; NHA/ABDM sources could not be reached to confirm a channel or timeline — treat as unverified. Penalties (DPDP Act 2023, Schedule, s.33; enforceable only once the relevant Act provisions commence on 13 May 2027): up to Rs 250 cr for failure of reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)); up to Rs 200 cr for failure to notify Board/affected Data Principals (s.8(6)); up to Rs 200 cr for children's obligations (s.9); up to Rs 150 cr for Significant Data Fiduciary obligations (s.10); up to Rs 10,000 for Data Principal duties (s.15); up to Rs 50 cr for any other breach. Each Schedule row is a separate head so cumulative exposure across heads is possible, but the Act does not use the word "stackable" and s.33 requires proportionality factors.

**Why it matters:** Build a breach register with timers for 6h/72h and templated notices to patients in their preferred language. Centralised, tamper-evident logging retained at least 180 days (1 year under DPDP Rule 8 after erasure) in an India region is a day-one infra requirement.

(c) Immutability: clinical data once entered is immutable ("As-Is principle"); updates/appends only with a full audit trail and "a new copy of data is created and original is retained through versioning". (d) Security requirements standard ISO/TS 14441:2013; automatic log-off after a predetermined inactivity period "must be forcibly terminated"; ISO 27799 for overall information-security management (plus IT Act 2000 and NABH); ISO 22600:2014 Parts 1-3 for privilege management/access control, with "Role Based, Policy Based, or Singular user" all acceptable (policy/rule-based preferred); audit log per ISO 27789:2013 recording date, time, record ID and user ID, plus an indication of the action, whenever records are created, modified (non-clinical data only), deleted (stale and non-clinical data only), or printed. (e) Integrity: "recommended that ... SHA-256 or higher must be used" for verifying data is not altered in transmission; encryption "as necessary according to organization preferences and best available encryption key strength" (web edition: minimum 256-bit key; no algorithm named; no explicit "encryption at rest" mandate); data exchange over encrypted, integrity-protected links using "HTTPS, SSL v3.0, and TLS v1.2" (SSL 3.0 is now obsolete - treat TLS 1.2+ as the floor); digital certificates/signing "recommended" per ISO 17090 Parts 1-5. (f) Preservation: health records "must compulsorily be preserved and not destroyed during the life-time of the person, ever"; records may be made inactive on death (no pending procedures/court cases), preferably under a "three (3) year rule" (inactive 3 years after death); permanent deletion strongly discouraged. (g) Interoperability: primary terminology SNOMED CT, with LOINC (tests/observations) and WHO ICD-10 (statistical reporting); imaging NEMA DICOM PS3.0-2015 (DIMSE + Part-10 files); scanned documents PDF/A-2 (ISO 19005-2); images JPEG at >=1024x768/300dpi; messaging HL7 V2.8.2-2015; e-prescriptions digitally signed by a registered practitioner.
