# Findings: competitors-india

Raw research output from the multi-agent sweep (October 2026). Each finding carries a verification tag:

- `confirmed` (5): passed independent fact-check
- `corrected` (19): fact-checker corrected the claim; text below is the corrected version
- `disputed` (1): two fact-checkers disagreed; treat as uncertain

## Summary

India's dental clinic software market is small in revenue (~USD 89M in 2025, 2.9% of the global DPMS market, growing ~13% CAGR to ~USD 242M by 2033 per Grand View) but very large in seats: the Dental Council of India register shows ~405,800 dentists (Part A), 329 colleges and ~28,000 BDS seats a year, with ORF projecting a 100,000+ dentist surplus over the next decade. The supply side is fragmented and poor: 65,000-125,000+ clinics depending on definition, more than 95% standalone, under 1% in chains (Clove ~715, Partha ~120-130, Sabka ~100-185 franchised, makeO ~30 own centres plus Apple Dental's 20, FMS 13). Dentist income is low and bimodal: a Kerala cohort study found 27% of recent graduates unemployed and 54.5% earning <=INR 15,000/month, while established owners earn INR 1-4 lakh/month. This caps willingness to pay at roughly INR 500-2,000/month for solo clinics, which is exactly the band the Indian market has converged on (Practo Ray INR 999-1,499/mo, KiviHealth INR 1,000/mo-equivalent, BestoSys INR 1,149-2,475/mo, Dontin INR 999-1,499/mo, Jaldee INR 9,999/yr, Lybrate INR 799/mo). Note: the regulator changed on 19 March 2026 - the DCI was dissolved and the National Dental Commission (NDC) now runs registration, so any "DCI-registered" language in the product must say NDC/State Dental Council.

Competitively there are three clusters. (1) Generic Indian clinic EMRs that dentists actually use because they are cheap and have mobile apps: Practo Ray (the default; INR 999-1,499/mo, but Practo Pro app sits at 3.8 stars/7.6K reviews with chronic support/refund complaints and a 7-year dentist review says tooth-surface charting is impossible), HealthPlix (free core app, INR 12-18K/yr paid, 16 specialties but dental not among the ones it markets, no offline), Eka Care EkaDoc (INR 2,999-27,999 per workspace, ABDM-first, WhatsApp Rx delivery), KiviHealth (INR 12,000+GST/yr, offline mode, lists dentists), Clinicea (INR 1,999-3,999/practitioner/mo, offline sync, but no mobile app), MocDoc (quote-based, has tooth chart, SMS not WhatsApp reminders, Chennai, strongest in South), DocPulse (quote-based, no dental module), Jaldee Health (INR 9,999/yr + INR 2,999/yr WhatsApp add-on + INR 4,999-9,999/yr for a mobile app), Lybrate (INR 799/clinic/mo). Zoylo is dead (deadpooled, app removed 2021). (2) Indian dental-specific vendors, mostly small, web-first and under-reviewed: Dentee (no published price, 15-day trial, 10K+ installs, reviewers flag missing WhatsApp), BestoSys (Pune; INR 1,149/1,485/2,475 per clinic/mo; real WhatsApp Business API; Android+iOS apps; ABDM only "on roadmap"), Dentsoftware (on-prem+cloud, INR 15-60K/yr, dated UX, weak WhatsApp, 25+ countries), DentSoft.in (browser-only, WhatsApp confirmations/reminders/PDF invoices, no tooth chart, no native app), Dontin, Adrine, Medical365, Smart Dental Desk, Toothclick (INR 12,099/yr), Track32, CAPS28, Dental Guru, Dentostack, Saral Dental Soft, Healcon. Dentalgraph and "Toothpick" could not be found as live products - the closest is Toothclick. (3) Chains build or import their own: Clove Dental's CIO states it evaluated 10+ EMRs and built an in-house web EMR-cum-CRM (point-of-sale style, big touch buttons) plus a "Clove Doctor" app; CareStack (Trivandrum-engineered, ~USD 79M raised, Straumann-backed) sells only in US/UK/AUS at USD 829-1,299/month and has no India offering, so it is a talent/benchmark reference, not a competitor at home.

The Play Store tells the mobile story: the most-installed dental clinic apps in India are not vendor SaaS at all but offline single-dentist utilities - "My Dental Clinic" (QuantumX, 100K+ installs, 4.2 stars/745 reviews, local backup/restore, PDF records) and "iDentist" (50K+, 3.8, complaints about photo sync across devices). Dentee sits at 10K+, Simpld 500+, Dentidesk 1K+, RCMS 100+. That means dentists want a phone-first, works-offline, one-tap tooth-charting tool, and nobody has paired that with cloud sync, WhatsApp automation, GST-correct billing and ABDM. Supporting evidence: 96% of Pune GDPs had WhatsApp and 72% said patients send queries on WhatsApp, 98.5% share radiographs/photos on it for second opinions, and 63% obtain no consent when doing so (JCDP 2018); 89% of 408 Vadodara dentists call digital dentistry "expensive" yet 95% would charge patients more for digital workflows (Bioinformation, Jan 2026); a national EHR survey found paper-only and hybrid record-keeping roughly tied, 47.5% fear data loss, 35.5% cite assistants' reluctance to type. Meta's India WhatsApp utility template rate is INR 0.115/message (marketing INR 0.86), so a reminder-heavy clinic costs the vendor well under INR 100/month in messaging - WhatsApp-native is economically trivial to bundle, yet Jaldee charges INR 2,999/yr for it and Practo Ray, Dentee, Clinicea and HealthPlix do not list it at all.

Gaps a dental-only, mobile-first, WhatsApp-native Indian product can own: (a) a true FDI-notation odontogram with surface-level charting and multi-sitting treatment plans on a phone (Practo Ray and most generics cannot record surfaces); (b) offline-first with cloud sync (only KiviHealth and Clinicea claim offline; the 100K-install apps are offline but no sync); (c) WhatsApp as the default channel for confirmations, post-op instructions, consent, estimates, PDF invoices and lab/second-opinion sharing with consent capture (addresses the 63%-no-consent problem); (d) GST-correct invoicing that splits exempt therapeutic services (SAC 9993) from taxable cosmetic work (AAR: bleaching/veneers 18%) and HSN 9021 implants/crowns at 5% - most vendors say "GST" without this nuance; (e) ABDM M1/M2 as table stakes by 2027 (Eka Care's wedge; BestoSys still on roadmap); (f) lab-work tracking and dental-specific inventory (implants, burs, alginate), which no generic EMR has; (g) pricing at INR 499-1,499/month with a free solo tier to undercut Practo's marketplace-fee model; (h) regional-language consent/Rx (HealthPlix does 14-22 languages for prescriptions; dental consent templates in Telugu/Hindi/Tamil are absent). Research caveats: the per-turn WebSearch budget was exhausted before I could verify Insta HMS and Medixcel pricing, Apollo White's clinic count, HealthPlix's Play Store rating, or scrape Play Store review text directly (Google's pages were truncated for the fetcher); those are listed under open questions.

### 1. `confirmed` · confidence high

India has ~405,800 registered dentists (Part A IDR count 405,777 plus 167 Part B) per the official register, concentrated in Karnataka (57,125), Maharashtra (55,672), Kerala (37,760), Tamil Nadu (36,824) and Andhra Pradesh (29,000). ORF (Nov 2025) cited 376,721, 329 dental colleges, 27,926 BDS seats/yr (82% private), a 1:~3,850 dentist-population ratio versus WHO's 1:7,500, and a projected surplus of 100,000+ dentists over the next decade; >70% of registered dentists are women.

**Why it matters:** The addressable base is ~4 lakh licences and growing >3x faster than population; the south (Karnataka, Kerala, TN, AP/Telangana) is the densest launch geography, and a female-majority, early-career, under-employed user base argues for a free/cheap solo tier and consumer-grade mobile UX rather than desktop enterprise software.

### 2. `confirmed` · confidence high

The Dental Council of India was dissolved on 19 March 2026; the National Dental Commission (NDC) with three boards (UG/PG Dental Education Board, Dental Assessment and Rating Board, Ethics and Dental Registration Board) now governs registration under the NDC Act 2023, and the Dentists Act 1948 is repealed.

**Why it matters:** Any registration-number field, prescription footer, consent form or marketing copy must reference NDC / State Dental Council rather than DCI; watch for new NDC rules on record-keeping, ethics and digital prescriptions that the app can be first to implement.

### 3. `corrected` · confidence medium

Clinic-count figures for India are poorly sourced and should not be presented as a consensus range. Nexdigm's India dental services report does NOT give a ~65,000 clinic figure; it states "over 300,000 practicing dentists", ~50,000 dental labs, and chain footprints (Clove Dental ~600 clinics; Apollo Dental 125+ centres across 25 cities as of early 2025). The Group Dentistry Now figure of "125,000+ private and government clinics" and "~95% of the market is individual dentists" are explicitly dated to 2011 (the year Clove was founded) in a January 2024 interview, not current data, and the article does not state "<1% chains". The "330,000+ practices" number is unsourced and appears to conflate the 300,000+ dentists headcount with practices. What can be supported: India has 300,000+ registered/practicing dentists, the market is overwhelmingly fragmented (roughly 95% individual practitioners per the 2011-era estimate, with chains like Clove ~600 and Apollo ~125 clinics being a tiny fraction), but no 2024-2026 source verified here gives an authoritative current clinic count. Software market sizing is correct: Nexdigm sizes India's dental software market at USD 89.5M (2024, no CAGR given); Grand View Research (page published 24 June 2026) puts India dental practice management software at USD 88.8M in 2025 (2.9% of global), USD 100.1M in 2026, growing at 13.4% CAGR (2026-2033) to USD 241.9M by 2033, with web-based deployment at 60.7% revenue share in 2025 and cloud-based the fastest-growing deployment mode.

**Why it matters:** The SAM is tens of thousands of solo/2-chair clinics, not hospitals; a product priced at INR 1,000/month capturing 10,000 clinics is ~INR 12 crore ARR, which is meaningful but means distribution cost per clinic must be very low (self-serve, referral, WhatsApp onboarding).
