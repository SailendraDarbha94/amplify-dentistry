# Findings: front-office-crm-billing

Raw research output from the multi-agent sweep (October 2026). Each finding carries a verification tag:

- `confirmed` (4): passed independent fact-check
- `corrected` (20): fact-checker corrected the claim; text below is the corrected version
- `disputed` (2): two fact-checkers disagreed; treat as uncertain

## Summary

Front-office economics in dentistry are dominated by three leaks that a dental-only product can attack directly: empty chair time (no-shows/cancellations), unscheduled treatment, and lapsed recall. The best dental-specific evidence is still the Sesame Communications analysis (1.6M appointments, 64 practices) showing automated multi-channel reminders cut no-shows 22.95%; broader systematic-review evidence shows reminders cut non-attendance ~34% relative (median DNA 23%→13%). 2025 US practice data puts cancellations at 14.4% and no-shows at 6.2%, with elite practices under 2%. Only 56% of presented treatment plans get scheduled and 46% completed, roughly 25% of an active base carries unscheduled treatment, and 25–40% of active patients are overdue for hygiene at any time. The KPI vocabulary dentists actually use is well settled: collections ratio ≥98% of adjusted production, AR>90 days <10%, hygiene reappointment ≥85–90%, case acceptance 60% average / 75–85% good, new patients 25–47/month, overhead 55–63%, supplies 5–7%, lab 6–10%. Scheduling is resource-based: separate doctor/hygiene/assistant columns on a 10-minute grid, procedure templates where assistant time overlaps so the doctor runs two columns (e.g., crown = 30 asst / 60 doc / 30 asst), and "rock/sand" block templates held ~3 days then released.

The India and US billing stacks diverge sharply, which is the core product-architecture decision. In the US, revenue cycle = ADA Dental Claim Form (2024 version adds 3a/11a payer IDs, 39a last-SRP date, 53a locum tenens), CDT codes, 837D claims through clearinghouses (DentalXChange ~$0.25/claim + $19.95/mo eligibility + $25/mo attachments; Vyne Trellis $99/location unlimited), 270/271 eligibility, 835 ERA posting, and patient financing via CareCredit (6–24 month no-interest on $200+, avg initial dental purchase $2,200) and in-house memberships (~$30/month, members visit 2–3x more). In India, dental is overwhelmingly self-pay: corporate plans cover only accident-related dental, OPD riders cap at ₹2,000–₹25,000 with 1–2 year waits and exclude scaling/bleaching/braces, cashless exists mainly via insurer tie-ups with chains (Clove, Apollo), and a nascent standalone dental plan market (₹1,500–₹3,500/yr) is emerging. So the India "insurance" module is really reimbursement documentation (itemized bill + prescription + certificate), while the money features are EMI (Bajaj Finserv Health EMI Network Card; Razorpay/Cashfree No-Cost EMI where the clinic bears 3–17% subvention), UPI (zero MDR), partial payments and running family ledgers.

Indian compliance is specific and affordable to build around: GST-exempt healthcare dentistry (Notification 12/2017 entry 74, SAC 999312) must be billed on a Bill of Supply, cosmetic work (bleaching, smile-design veneers; SAC 999722 per 2022 Maharashtra AAR) on a Tax Invoice — and Notification 15/2025-CT(R) moved all of Group 99972 ("beauty and physical well-being services") to a mandatory 5% without ITC from 22 Sept 2025, which likely replaces the earlier 18% reading for cosmetic dentistry (needs CA confirmation). Patient communication costs are tiny but regulated: WhatsApp utility messages ₹0.115 and marketing ₹0.8631 (+18% GST, +BSP markup), service replies free until 1 Oct 2026 then 1,000 free/number/month; SMS needs one-time DLT entity registration (~₹5,900), -T/-S/-P header suffixes since the Feb 2025 TCCCPR amendment, and promotional traffic only 9am–9pm to non-DND numbers. E-signed consent is valid under the IT Act 2000 (Second Schedule, Aadhaar eSign) if identity, sole control and an audit trail are provable. The Indian PMS market prices at ₹399–₹3,000/clinic/month, with clinics asking for GST billing, official WhatsApp API, DPDP readiness, regional languages and multi-branch; Clove (715 clinics, ₹377.7 Cr FY25 revenue, ₹165 Cr loss) and Sabka Dentist (100–110 clinics) define the chain segment. Note: the web-search budget ran out before I could research referral tracking, treatment-plan presentation tooling, DPDP rules timing, insurance-verification labour time and India's hygienist workforce; those are listed as open questions.

## Findings

### 1. `corrected` · confidence high

A vendor-run Sesame Communications analysis (reported in Dental Tribune US, print Feb 2013 / online 19 June 2013) of 1,604,184 appointments across 64 US dental practices over five years reported that automated email/text/voice reminders cut no-shows by 22.95% versus pre-activation, with $31,456.88 of incremental production per practice; the article says production benefits "continued throughout the 36-month post-activation period" but does not state that the $31,457 figure is a 36-month total. It also reports 79.5% of patients preferring SMS/email reminders over phone calls; the "97% would rather click than call" figure comes from a separate Sesame "national research study", not the appointment dataset. The article calls it a "breakthrough study", not the "largest" dental reminder study — no evidence supports the "largest" label. Methodology is a pre/post comparison with no control group and no published peer-reviewed paper; Sesame is now a Henry Schein One brand, so the result is a vendor marketing claim. All figures are US-only (USD production, US practices) from 2008-2012-era data and say nothing about Indian dental clinics, where WhatsApp rather than SMS/email/voice dominates patient messaging and fee levels are far lower; treat it as directional evidence that reminders reduce no-shows, not as a benchmark for an Indian product.

**Why it matters:** Automated multi-channel reminders with patient-chosen channel are table stakes; expect ~20-25% no-show reduction, not the 40-50% some vendors claim. Build channel preference per patient (WhatsApp/SMS/email/call).

### 2. `corrected` · confidence medium

Hasvold & Wootton's 2011 systematic review (J Telemed Telecare 17(7):358-64; 29 studies/33 estimates, hospital appointments, literature from 1999 onward, mostly non-dental, non-Indian settings) found reminders reduced non-attendance in all but one study, with a weighted mean relative reduction of about 34% of baseline; manual phone calls (~39% reduction) outperformed automated SMS/calls (~29%); timing (day before vs week before) made no clear difference; average cost ~EUR 0.41 per reminder. The "median no-show 23% -> 13%" figure is NOT from that review: it comes from Parikh et al. 2010 (Am J Med 123(6):542-8), a single US academic outpatient clinic where no-show was ~23% with no reminder, ~17% with automated reminders and ~14% with staff phone calls. The orthodontic figures are from Wegrzyniak et al. 2018 (Angle Orthod 88(3):314-8): 1,193 appointments at one US private orthodontic office (2015 data, 6 months), patient-chosen reminder method, overall no-show 2.43%; SMS 1.90%, e-mail 2.68%, phone 3.49%; not statistically significant (P = .569); patients chose e-mail 53.1%, SMS 39.6%, phone 7.2%. The "adding a daily reminder to a weekly one raises confirmations a further 26%" statistic is an uncited vendor claim on Dialog Health's blog (a reminder-software seller, acquired by SpinSci in July 2026) with no identifiable study behind it and should not be presented as research. None of these studies involve Indian dental clinics or WhatsApp; no India-specific evidence was found in the stated sources.

**Why it matters:** Design a default cadence of 7 days + 24-48h + morning-of, with two-way confirm ('Reply 1 to confirm'); escalate unconfirmed high-value slots to a human call since manual calls still outperform automation.

### 4. `corrected` · confidence medium

The listed benchmarks are the typical rules of thumb published by US/Canadian dental billing and marketing vendors, but the claim overstates their provenance and mixes incompatible figures. Corrections: (1) None of the three stated sources cites the ADA; the 98% collections target is a vendor convention (Dental Billing Assist, May 2026: target 98%+, "industry average" 91-94%, unattributed; Dentx: average 95%, good 98%+; Sunrise: average ~95%, target 98%+), and the ADA HPI practice-research page gives only net income/gross billings (2025: $215,320 / $965,660 for GPs), not collection or AR targets. (2) The "average" figures are internally inconsistent across sources: AR>90 days average is 15-25% (DBA) vs 8% (Dentx); collections average 91-94% (DBA) vs 95% (Dentx/Sunrise). (3) Days in AR <30 (best 18-24, avg 32-45), denial rate <5% (avg 10-15%), clean-claim >=95% (avg 80-85%), bad debt >2% as a concern all come solely from Dental Billing Assist's unsourced table and its own client data; Sunrise uses a looser "first-pass approval >90%" and AR days 30-45. (4) Hygiene share of production is 28-33% per Dentx but 40-50% per Sunrise; production per hour is $450 avg / $600+ good per Dentx but $300-500 per Sunrise; new patients 25 avg / 40+ good is Dentx only (Sunrise: "varies by practice size"). (5) Hygiene reappointment benchmark in both sources is 85%+, not "85-90%". (6) Overhead 55-63% with staff 20-24%, supplies 5-6%, lab 6-7%, facilities 5-7%, marketing 3-5% comes from Dentx alone, which states its numbers are "compiled industry ranges...not a single published dataset" and gives conflicting overhead bands (65% avg vs 55-60% good vs <55%). (7) All figures are USD/CAD and assume a US-style insurance-claims model (clean claims, denials, PPO fees); none applies to India, where most dental payments are cash/UPI out-of-pocket and insurance claims are marginal, so denial/clean-claim/days-in-AR KPIs are largely irrelevant to an India-first product and no Indian benchmark data was found in the stated sources. Use these as US-market reference ranges with explicit vendor attribution, not as industry- or ADA-validated standards.

**Why it matters:** These exact ratios should be first-class, pre-defined reports (with the formula shown) rather than user-built; multi-location rollups should compute them per provider and per branch. India needs rupee-based production/hour and no insurance-adjusted production by default.

### 26. `confirmed` · confidence low
