# Findings: clinical-workflows

Raw research output from the multi-agent sweep (October 2026). Each finding carries a verification tag:

- `confirmed` (12): passed independent fact-check
- `corrected` (11): fact-checker corrected the claim; text below is the corrected version
- `disputed` (5): two fact-checkers disagreed; treat as uncertain
- `unverified-low-risk` (2): not fact-checked (judged low-risk)

## Summary

Dentistry's clinical record is organised around a spatial object (the tooth, its five surfaces and six periodontal sites) rather than around encounter narrative, and that single fact explains most of what generic EHRs get wrong. The mature dental PMS data model (best documented publicly by Open Dental's manual) treats every procedure as a line item with a treatment area (tooth / surface / quadrant / arch / tooth-range / mouth), a tooth number in one of four nomenclatures (Universal, FDI, Palmer, Haderup), a surface string (M, O/I, D, B/F, L, plus a Class V flag that must be stripped on claims), a status lifecycle (TP -> TPi / Complete / Existing-other-provider / Referred / Condition) and a priority (phase) within an Active / Inactive / Saved treatment plan. Perio is a separate 192-site matrix (PD, gingival margin with recession stored as a negative, auto-CAL = PD + GM, MGJ, furcation 0-3, mobility, bleeding/suppuration/plaque/calculus flags) with multi-exam overlay, skip-teeth and implant handling, and screening indices (UK BPE 0-4/*, US PSR) that gate when a full 6-point chart is required. Mixed dentition (a position holding both a primary and a permanent tooth), supernumeraries (Universal +50, FDI 99), eruption/exfoliation states, and age-based default dentition are where even the market leaders are weak: Open Dental cannot perio-chart primary or supernumerary teeth and requires marking unerupted permanent molars as "primary" as a workaround.

For an India-first product the coding and regulatory picture is distinctive. India has no national dental procedure code set; the operative regulation is the DCI Revised Dentists' Code of Ethics 2014, which requires records retained at least 3 years, copies within 72 hours, registration number on every prescription/certificate/receipt, cost estimates before treatment, generic prescribing "as far as possible", and "efforts to digitalise" records. CDT (US) is ADA-copyrighted and needs a negotiated per-product commercial licence even when codes are embedded invisibly (processing 2-4 weeks, up to 2 months); SNODENT and the SNODENT-ICD-10-CDT maps are separately licensed. The 2020 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines explicitly cover only RMPs under the IMC Act 1956, so dentists have no formal teledentistry e-prescribing framework; its List O/A/B structure is still the obvious template, and the Drugs & Cosmetics Rules' Schedule H/H1 (prescription-only; H1 requires a register) applies to the amoxicillin/metronidazole/NSAID prescribing that dominates Indian dental practice. Consent should be captured per procedure with tooth-number merge fields and an IT Act Second-Schedule signature (Aadhaar eSign) where legal weight matters, with DPDP Act 2023/Rules 2025 consent and minors rules layered on top.

Ancillary dental workflows that a niche product should treat as first-class modules, each with its own data model: lab cases (lab, per-service turnaround that computes due date skipping holidays, shade, Created/Sent/Received/Quality-Checked timestamps, slip PDF); implants (UDI-DI + lot/serial captured at placement; EU MDR Art. 18 implant card since 26 May 2021 and CED says dental implants are not exempt); sterilisation (CDC: mechanical every load, chemical indicator in every pack, biological weekly and every load with an implantable; IDA mirrors this; NABH DHSP and BMW Rules 2016 apply in India); medical-history red flags with structured risk outputs (SDCEP MRONJ low/higher risk table that must be recorded in notes, SDCEP anticoagulant/DOAC rules, NICE CG19 recall intervals 3-24 months); endodontic per-canal records (reference point, WL by EAL + radiograph, MAF, obturation length/density/sealer); orthodontic visit grids with aligner stage/tray counting; and imaging that is tooth-linked rather than attachment-based, acquired via TWAIN from RVG sensors and stored as DICOM IO/DX, knowing that DICOM still has no standard way to say which teeth are in an intraoral image.

Open-source reuse is thin for Flutter: pub.dev has no odontogram or perio-chart package, so the tooth chart must be built in-house (CustomPainter/SVG), but two MIT React components (react-advanced-odontogram with FHIR R4 export, ICDAS and perio; biomathcode/react-odontogram with FDI/Universal/Palmer and a React Native path) are good references for the state model, and the HL7 Dental Data Exchange IG (ADA 1084) defines the interoperability target (Dental Condition / Dental Finding / Dental Service Request profiles, SNODENT/SNOMED tooth sites, LOINC measurements). Note: the shared web-search budget ran out before I could research treatment-plan case-acceptance benchmarks, PSR specifics, and AERB radiology registration for Indian clinics; those are listed as open questions.

## Findings

### 1. `corrected` · confidence high

Three tooth-numbering systems should be supported with one canonical stored form: FDI two-digit (ISO 3950; quadrant digit 1-4 permanent, 5-8 primary, e.g. 11/21) is the international system used by WHO/FDI and the overwhelmingly likely clinical default in India, though no survey of Indian usage was found to cite; Universal (1-32 permanent, A-T primary) is the US/ADA system, enumerated as an HL7 code system (ADA Universal Tooth Designation System v1.0.1, 2024) that lists supernumerary permanent 51-82 but only nine supernumerary deciduous codes (AS, BS, CS, DS, ES, FS, GS, IS, JS), not AS-TS; Palmer (Zsigmondy-Palmer, quadrant bracket + 1-8 / A-E) is still preferred by UK dental students, practitioners and orthodontists, but the only cited evidence is a 1998 UK dental-school survey (Blinkhorn et al.), and Harris (2005) already called it "largely of historical interest". Open Dental's Tooth Nomenclature preference offers Universal, FDI, Haderup (Danish) and Palmer; teeth display in Palmer only when viewing from the Ortho tab with the separate "Tooth chart ortho mode" preference enabled; Universal supernumeraries are tooth+50 (51-82) or letter+S (AS-TS), FDI users enter 99, and supernumerary teeth are not drawn on the graphical chart. Store teeth internally in one canonical code (e.g. FDI or an internal ID) and render in the practice's chosen system.

**Why it matters:** Store an internal canonical tooth ID and render FDI/Universal/Palmer as a display preference per clinic and per specialist (Indian GP = FDI, orthodontist = Palmer); model supernumerary and primary teeth from day one or the chart will break for pediatric and ortho users.

### 2. `unverified-low-risk` · confidence high

Procedure line items carry a 'treatment area' (single tooth, tooth range, quadrant, arch, surface, mouth) and a surface string built from buttons B/F, V, M, O/I, D, L. 'V' marks a Class V gingival-margin lesion, is drawn separately on the chart, is not an allowed claim surface and is converted to B/F on all claims (DBV -> DB), and is not counted as an extra surface for auto-codes.

**Why it matters:** Your procedure schema needs treatment_area enum + tooth + surfaces[] + the ability to collapse V into B/F for billing/insurance export; surface-count-driven fee rules (1-surface vs 3-surface composite) depend on this normalisation.

### 3. `corrected` · confidence high

A full periodontal chart in Open Dental is a 6-site x 32-tooth matrix (192 possible sites, less any teeth marked skipped) recording per site probing depth, gingival margin (entered values are implied negative = recession; a positive margin coronal to the CEJ is entered via the "Ging Marg+" checkbox or Ctrl+number), a read-only auto-CAL computed from probing plus gingival margin, mucogingival junction (charted on maxillary facial and mandibular facial/lingual only), furcation 0-3, one mobility value per tooth (facial row only), and per-site plaque/calculus/bleeding/suppuration flags (keys P/C/B/S, shown as coloured dots). Open Dental supports SkipTeeth (persisting to the next exam and skipped by auto-advance), implants shown with an "i" suffix such as 17i (optionally treated as missing via a preference), a "Set All 323" button plus a "Default" button that adds a new exam with configurable default depths, two keyboard auto-advance sequences (Maxillary First and Facials First), display of up to SIX exams in total for comparison (the current exam plus up to FIVE earlier exams greyed out, not six prior exams), a "Calc Index %" button that recalculates FOUR index percentages (bleeding, plaque, calculus, suppuration; rounded down, e.g. 19/192 = 10%), and global "Red if" threshold highlighting for probing, gingival margin, CAL, furcation and mobility (MGJ is flagged when at or below the threshold; thresholds apply to all patients). It can chart only permanent teeth; supernumerary and retained deciduous teeth cannot be marked on the chart and go in the exam note. Note for an India-first product: the Open Dental manual describes the chart in Universal numbering (1-32), whereas Indian and UK practice (and the Pabau/SDCEP sources) use FDI notation (e.g. tooth 18), and the SDCEP/BSP convention grades furcation 1-3 and mobility 0-3; the 6-site, PD + recession = CAL model itself is universal and not India-specific.

**Why it matters:** This is the minimum perio data model; recession sign convention must be fixed in the schema (SDCEP: CAL = PD + recession) or auto-CAL will be wrong; mobile entry needs auto-advance and voice/assistant call-out to be usable chairside; supporting primary/supernumerary perio sites is a differentiation gap even against Open Dental.

### 4. `confirmed` · confidence high

The UK BSP Basic Periodontal Examination (Jan 2019, review Jan 2024) screens 6 sextants with a WHO probe (0.5 mm ball, black band 3.5-5.5 mm, 20-25 g force): codes 0/1/2 (<3.5 mm; 1 = bleeding, 2 = calculus/overhang), 3 (3.5-5.5 mm), 4 (>5.5 mm), '*' furcation; a sextant needs >=2 teeth; code 3 triggers a 6-point chart of that sextant after initial therapy, code 4 triggers full-mouth 6-point charting; only sites >=4 mm need recording; BOP always recorded with 6PPC; BPE must not be used around implants; radiographs for all code 3/4 sextants. The 2017 AAP/EFP classification stages periodontitis I-IV and grades A-C, and the BSP maps BPE to it.

**Why it matters:** Build a screening index (BPE for UK/India academic users, PSR for US) that automatically unlocks/prompts the full 6-point chart per sextant, blocks BPE on implant sites, and lets the clinician record a 2017 stage/grade diagnosis; this is a real workflow accelerator a generic EHR cannot offer.

### 5. `corrected` · confidence high

Open Dental's treatment-plan model (manual v24.3/v24.4, current as of Oct 2026): exactly one Active plan per patient (procedures attached to scheduled appointments are always on it; completed procedures are removed from it), multiple Inactive plans whose procedures may be TP or TPi (TPi procedures are hidden from the Chart Module/tooth chart/Active plan; Inactive plans cannot be signed), and Saved plans created "to create permanent record" of accepted treatment. Saved plans are NOT strictly immutable: their fees are frozen (unaffected by the Update Fees tool) and procedure order is fixed, but an unsigned saved plan's date, heading, note, responsible party, presenter and individual procedures can still be edited, and the saved plan can be deleted; only once it is electronically signed ("Sign TP") does it become uneditable, and even then the signature can be cleared to re-enable edits. Saved plans can be pushed to a patient tablet via eClipboard (QR/unlock code, side-by-side comparison of several saved plans, on-device signing; signed PDFs auto-save to Imaging only with a preference enabled and non-local image storage). Phasing uses customizable priorities defined in Definitions > Treat' Plan Priorities ("numbers, letters, or words up to 7 characters", e.g., Sched, Wait, Next, ?, Decline, Last, Low, High, NewYear, Urgent, WaitIns, Altern, Plan A, Plan B, RecPlan, AltPlan) with per-priority colors, sort order and hiding, and optional "Subtotals for each priority level". Grid columns include Tth, Surf, Code, Dx, Prognosis, Prov, Priority, Fee, Pri Ins, Sec Ins, Discounts, Pat; Pat = Fee - Pri Ins - Sec Ins - Discount, where the Discounts column = procedure discounts + PPO insurance write-offs. Note this is a US-insurance-centric model (primary/secondary insurance estimates, PPO write-offs); an India-first product would need the same Active/Inactive/Saved + priority-phasing structure but with a cash/package-pricing patient-portion model rather than Pri/Sec Ins columns.

**Why it matters:** Alternatives (Plan A vs Plan B: RCT+crown vs extraction+implant) and phased plans are core to dental case presentation; model plan versions as immutable signed snapshots plus a live active plan, with per-phase subtotals and discount/insurance estimates, and a tablet 'present and sign' flow.

### 6. `confirmed` · confidence high

CDT (Code on Dental Procedures and Nomenclature) is ADA-copyrighted; any commercial product that bundles, interprets or manipulates CDT codes, even when the codes are not visible to users (claims, reports, analytics), needs an ADA commercial licence with per-product royalties set in a template agreement; application processing takes 2-4 weeks and up to 2 months; SNODENT and the SNODENT->ICD-10-CM (2-way) and SNODENT->ICD-10-CM->CDT (3-way) maps are separate licences released each October; contact CDT-SNODENT@ada.org. No price list is published. CDT 2026 added 31 codes, deleted 6 and revised ~12-14.

**Why it matters:** Budget legal/licensing time before any US launch; design the procedure catalogue so the India/UK editions ship with a clinic-defined (non-CDT) catalogue and CDT is a licensed plug-in; plan an annual October code-set update job.

### 7. `corrected` · confidence medium

SNODENT (Systematized Nomenclature of Dentistry) is an ADA-owned dental diagnostic terminology that the ADA describes as an ANSI-approved American National Standard, maintained through ADA Standards Consensus Body 3 (Terminology & SNODENT), containing a browsable hierarchy of 7,000+ terms, updated every fall, and "harmonized/synchronized with SNOMED-CT" (the exact ANSI approval date and the "caries -> enamel/dentin" hierarchy example could not be verified from accessible sources as of Oct 2026; the stated ADA News article page did not render). On June 19, 2012 the ADA and IHTSDO (renamed SNOMED International in 2017) signed a licensing agreement allowing IHTSDO to incorporate SNODENT codes into the SNOMED CT International Release via a Dental Special Interest Group; the ADA today frames this as synchronization, not full containment. The ADA offers SNODENT usage licenses at no cost to users, and licenses a 2-way map (SNODENT -> ICD-10-CM) and a 3-way map (SNODENT -> ICD-10-CM -> CDT) to commercial users under royalty-bearing template agreements (no public price; files released annually in October; applications take 2-4 weeks, up to 2 months). In Open Dental, SNOMED CT is used primarily on Problems (a problem must carry a SNOMED CT code to appear on a CCD and for CQM/CDS features); on patient allergies the only SNOMED CT field is the optional "SNOMED CT Reaction", sent in the CCD XML, while the allergen itself is coded via UNII (ingredient) or RxNorm (medication). Open Dental's EHR/CCD features are legacy (attestation unsupported after 2018). All of this (SNODENT, CDT, CCD) is US-specific infrastructure and carries no regulatory weight in India.

**Why it matters:** For diagnosis coding use SNOMED CT (free in India via SNOMED International member licence? verify) or ICD-10 K00-K14 as the default, and treat SNODENT as a US add-on; keep a 'diagnosis per tooth/surface' field (Open Dental has Dx and Prognosis columns) because payers and the HL7 dental IG expect it.
