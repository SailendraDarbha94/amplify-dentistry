# Imaging reality in Indian dental clinics

Which sensors, software and formats Indian 1-5 chair clinics actually use, how X-rays reach WhatsApp today, AERB registration, and a ranked ingestion architecture for a phone-first app.
> Part of the [dental practice-management research](README.md) (October 2026). This report comes from a dedicated research agent run after the main sweep; its findings carry the agent's own confidence tags and were not put through the adversarial fact-check pass.

## Summary

Indian 1–5 chair clinics image almost entirely with a USB CMOS intraoral sensor ("RVG") plugged into a Windows laptop or desktop; OPG is a second-tier purchase and CBCT is nearly always referred out to an imaging centre. A 2025 survey of 155 Indian dentists/students found RVG the most-used modality (67.1%) and CBCT the least (5.8%), with 80.6% having used RVG and only 39.4% having a CBCT in their setup (sample skewed to colleges). Street prices sort into three tiers: Chinese entry sensors at ₹49k–75k (Woodpecker i-Sensor ₹52–67k; Waldent RTA/Carpo ₹51–56k; Eighteeth NanoPix ₹52–73k; Xpect Vision ₹49–54k), mid-tier Korean/French at ₹77k–1.4 lakh (Vatech EzSensor Classic 1.5, Carestream RVG 142/5200), and premium at ₹1.5–2.25 lakh (Carestream RVG 6200 ₹1,85,130 ex-GST official bundle, Acteon Sopix, Dentsply Xios XG Supreme). Dexis Titanium has no INR street listing. Distribution is direct (Carestream Dental India, Vatech India, Dentsply India) plus marketplaces (Dentalkart/Vasa Denticity, Medikabazaar, IndiaMART) and Unicorn DenMart (Genoray, Xpect Vision, Rayscan). Prevest DenPro sells materials, not imaging; Confident Dental makes AERB-licensed X-ray generators, not sensors.

Every sensor sold in India is wired-USB and Windows-only; wireless sensors (Xios XG WiFi, Carestream RVG 6500/iPad) exist globally but Indian dealers state only the USB variant is sold here. The bundled software (CS Imaging 8, EzDent-i, Woodpecker Ai-Dental, Sidexis 4, Romexis) stores images in proprietary databases and exposes three escape hatches: (1) a manual Save-As/Export to JPEG/PNG/BMP/TIFF, (2) DICOM export or C-STORE in the Carestream/Planmeca/Sirona tier only, and (3) TWAIN. The DICOM conformance statements show the premium vendors send intraoral images uncompressed (Explicit/Implicit VR Little Endian) while CBCT exports from Vatech Ez3D-i are "Lossless" (JPEG Lossless/JPEG 2000); CS Imaging 8 also supports DICOMweb to a web PACS, MWL and MPPS. No vendor publishes a public SDK; Sidexis SLIDA and Vatech's linkage.xml bridge are partner/file-based.

There is no documented study of how Indian dentists move an RVG into WhatsApp, but the vendor help pages, forum workarounds (Paint screenshot), proprietary .RVG files, and Indian PMS marketing ("instantly share Dental X-rays via WhatsApp") all point to the same two-step manual path: export or screenshot to JPEG on the chairside PC, then WhatsApp Web or a phone transfer. The friction is patient matching, file naming, the PC-to-phone hop, and WhatsApp compression. Phone photography is already the norm for clinical photos: 77.6% of surveyed Tamil Nadu dentists use a phone camera versus 22.4% DSLR, with consent mostly verbal (75.7%). Regulation is lighter than most founders assume: dental units (intraoral, handheld, OPG, CBCT) fall under AERB "Registration" via the eLORA Dental Radiology module, not the CT/IR "Licence"; layout approval is not applicable for intraoral/handheld units; TLD badges are "recommendatory" for dental; RSO is self-designated; the certificate is now valid 10 years; QA is due every 2 years. But AERB can seal unregistered units under Rule 31 and prosecute under the Atomic Energy Act (up to 5 years), which makes a certificate vault plus QA/renewal reminders a cheap, credible feature.

## Findings

### [high] RVG is the universal chairside modality; OPG is secondary; CBCT is referred out
A 2025 Indian questionnaire (n=155) found 80.6% had used RVG, RVG was the most-used modality (104, 67.1%) and CBCT the least (9, 5.8%); institutions "usually have a digital dental X-ray machine, less often a digital panoramic X-ray machine, and least often a CBCT unit"; most clinicians prescribe 1–5 CBCTs/month, mainly for implants (78.1%). Imaging centres (e.g., Stellar Dental Imagings, Mumbai) deliver DICOM to referring dentists "via email or our secure cloud portal", or on pendrive/CD, with a PDF report that is what actually travels on WhatsApp.
- Why it matters: the phone-first app must nail intraoral 2D first; CBCT support is an import-and-view problem (DICOM from a centre), not a capture problem.

### [medium] The real-world RVG-to-WhatsApp path is export-or-screenshot to JPEG, then WhatsApp Web; no Indian study documents it, but every artefact points there
Evidence chain: (a) native formats are proprietary (.RVG, CS "current format", EzDent-i DB); (b) vendor/forum guidance is "File > Save As / Export" to JPG/PNG or paste into Paint and "Save as JPEG picture"; (c) Indian PMS vendors sell the gap: Drlogy "Instantly share prescriptions, Dental X-rays, and treatment reports via WhatsApp"; DentSoft "easy uploading of patient images including X Ray, Panoramic, Intra oral"; a Capterra reviewer of Dentee: "one thing which is lacking is integration with whatsapp"; Medecro.ai "upload RVG, OPG" for AI reports; (d) WhatsApp compresses images and will not carry DICOM folders. Diagnostic studies show smartphone screen-photos/WhatsApp images are acceptable for panoramic third-molar and marginal-gap reading but less accurate than PACS in a radiology registrar study.
- Why it matters: friction is patient matching, file naming, PC-to-phone hop and compression; the app wins by collapsing export + match + send into one tap, and by keeping a diagnostic-quality copy alongside the WhatsApp JPEG.

### [high] AERB: dental units are "Registration" on the eLORA Dental Radiology module, not a CT-style Licence; the clinic owner self-serves it
AERB "Guidelines for e-LORA module of Dental Radiology users" (2019): Step 1 institute registration (credentials by email in "4-5 working days" per Carestream's guide, "about 10 days" per Alerio); indigenous equipment = 2 steps, imported = 3 steps (procurement certificate, then "Application for Registration of New Import Dental X-ray Equipment"); form tabs: Employee details ("PMS no recommendatory for dental users. One person from your institute should be designated as RSO"), Equipment details ("select the type approved model from the drop down... select the authorized supplier/service agency who has performed the QA tests"), protective accessories and "leakage level as mentioned in QA report". Approval email: "Your application for obtaining Licence for operation has been approved" with the Registration certificate in "My Casefile". AERB's eLORA guide: RSO approval "mandatory only for Licence category x-ray equipment i.e. Computed Tomography and Interventional Radiology"; older AERB table: for intra-oral dental, layout "Not Applicable", requirements = NOC/Type Approval copy, RSO approval, PMS availability, QA report. Equipment types recognised: Dental (Intra-oral), Dental (Hand-held), OPG, CBCT. CDSCO Class C registration of X-ray machines is a supplier obligation since 1 Jan 2021.

## Recommended ingestion architecture (ranked options with reasons)

1. **Windows watch-folder agent (primary, ship first).** A tiny signed .NET/Go tray app paired to the clinic by QR; watches one or more export folders (the vendor's default JPEG/PNG/DICOM export folder plus a "Drop here" folder on the desktop), normalises files (DICOM → 16-bit PNG/JPEG preview + original kept; JPEG → stripped EXIF), infers patient/tooth from filename and from "last opened patient on the phone" (the dentist taps the patient in the app, then exports), and queues to a local SQLite outbox. Flaky internet: the agent first publishes on the LAN (mDNS/HTTP) so the phone/front desk pull images in seconds over clinic Wi-Fi, then background-syncs to cloud with resumable chunked uploads. Works with every vendor found (CS Imaging Save As/drag-drop JPEG, EzDent-i export, Ai-Dental, Romexis Viewer, Sidexis Sidicom RM/SLIDA partners, i-Scan). Cost: the dentist still presses Export once per image.
2. **DICOM C-STORE listener inside the same agent (premium tier, cheap add-on).** Expose an AE title on localhost:11112 so CS Imaging 8, Romexis, Sidexis-via-partner and OPG/CBCT consoles auto-send on acquisition with zero clicks; also a cloud STOW-RS endpoint for CS Imaging 8's DICOMweb path. Parse Explicit/Implicit VR LE first; add JPEG Lossless P14 and JPEG 2000 for CBCT exports; keep MWL out of v1. Store original DICOM for the pure-Dart viewer, derive an 8-bit JPEG for WhatsApp.
3. **Web front desk drag-and-drop, paste (Ctrl+V screenshot) and folder-picker.** Zero-install fallback for shared PCs; Chrome's File System Access API can watch a chosen folder in-browser on Windows. Same normaliser and patient-match UI as the agent.
4. **Phone photo of the monitor with perspective correction and grey normalisation.** Ubiquitous, offline, and already what dentists do; label it "non-diagnostic copy" in metadata, keep it tooth-linked, and nudge to the agent for a diagnostic-quality copy. Research shows acceptable patient-communication quality but lower accuracy than PACS.
5. **Direct phone camera for intraoral/extraoral photos.** Primary photo path (77.6% already use phones); add guides for retractor/mirror shots, auto before/after pairing per tooth, consent capture.
6. **TWAIN acquisition mode (phase 2, selective).** A 32-bit host process inside the agent that drives Woodpecker/Carestream/Vatech TWAIN sources so the dentist captures from your UI; eliminates vendor software but is driver-fragile (Woodpecker TWAIN "unreliable"), so gate it behind per-model certification.
7. **Wi-Fi/mobile RVG and PSP units.** Only Woodpecker i-Scan (chair-side mode, mobile viewing) is relevant in India; treat as a folder/export integration. Wireless Xios/RVG 6500 are not sold here.
8. **Scanner/film apps.** Conventional IOPA/OPG film persists in tier-2/3 towns; reuse option 4 against a viewbox with a "film" flag rather than building a flatbed path.

For a solo clinic with flaky internet the non-negotiables are: LAN-first delivery, a durable outbox on both PC and phone, idempotent uploads keyed by content hash, and never blocking the chairside flow on cloud availability.
