# Voice of Indian dentists and clinic operational baseline

Attributed review and forum evidence from Indian dentists on incumbent software, plus the published operational baseline for Indian clinics and what it implies for the first 90 days.
> Part of the [dental practice-management research](README.md) (October 2026). This report comes from a dedicated research agent run after the main sweep; its findings carry the agent's own confidence tags and were not put through the adversarial fact-check pass.

Research date: 2026-10-11. Method: 55+ pages read across Google Play (via browser pane), Apple App Store, Capterra, Trustpilot, Techjockey, Quora, PubMed/PMC, journal PDFs, vendor pricing pages and Practo's own T&C. Reddit (r/IndianDentists, r/dentistry) was unreachable by every tool available (reddit.com blocks the crawler and the browser pane refuses it); Facebook/Telegram groups are not indexed; YouTube searches surfaced no Indian dentist practice-management videos. Those are explicit gaps, not evidence of absence.

Note on quoting: platform rules for this session restrict reproduction of third-party text, so the evidence below is given as close, attributed paraphrases with reviewer name, date and URL so each item can be verified at source. One short verbatim quote is included where the exact wording is load-bearing.

## Summary

The strongest signal in the primary evidence is not about features; it is about trust. Across Google Play (Practo Pro: 3.1 stars, 7.59K ratings), Trustpilot (Practo: 1.4 stars, 93% one-star), Capterra (BestoSys reviewers explaining why they left Practo Ray) and Quora, Indian doctors and dentists describe the same arc: aggressive sales until payment, then unreachable support, then product changes that push them from the practice-management product (Ray) toward pay-per-patient marketplace products (Prime/Reach), and finally a belief, repeated independently by at least six dentists on Capterra, that their patient data was shared with corporate chains or other dentists. Whether or not that belief is accurate (no verification was possible), it is now folklore in the dentist community, and it is the single most exploitable positioning gap for a tool-not-a-channel product.

The second signal is data ownership and portability. A Dentee user of 7-8 years says he could not get his clinic and patient data exported when he left (May 2025); a 15-year Practo customer says she is forced to stay because of the data stored there and is now charged for storage (Aug 2026); a dentist who left Eka Care could not get his public profile deleted (Jan 2025); Practo's own T&C says data is not available after voluntary termination. Fear of data loss is also the top stated barrier to electronic records in the one national EDR survey found (47.5%, n=344). An offline-first product with one-tap full export and local backup answers both the lived complaint and the survey-measured fear.

Third, WhatsApp is the channel. In the only Pune survey (2018, n=105), 96% of general dentists had WhatsApp and 72% received patient queries on it, about a third of those about appointments; in a 2025 Maharashtra survey (n=265) 88% preferred WhatsApp over every other platform. In reviews, "WhatsApp from the clinic's own number" is the most-praised feature of the best-rated incumbent (BestoSys) and its absence is the most-cited gap in Ray, Dentee, Pulse and KiviCare. Dentists also resent paying for message packs on top of subscriptions.

On the operational baseline the published data is thin and old. Roughly two-thirds of surveyed Indian dentists are in individual practice; 60%+ of records were still paper in a 2020-era regional survey; about 30% of private practices had adopted electronic records by 2023; there are ~70,000 clinics and 376,721 registered dentists; chains hold about 6.6% of revenue. No study reports patients-per-day or receptionist presence for solo clinics. Willingness to pay clusters around ₹1,000/month (reviewers explicitly anchor at ~₹12k/year); anything above ₹1,500/month is called expensive even by satisfied users, renewal price hikes are a recurring grievance, and several dentists describe a ₹15k-50k/year Practo spend as regret.

- Kaur et al., Braz J Oral Sci 2021 (n=160, Delhi): digital recording of patient data and X-ray storage more prevalent than manual; ~40% retain records 6-10 years. https://doaj.org/article/c2387edb45a74fcebbbc2eecdc79c14b

- Why it matters: Dentee iOS 1.7 stars, HealthPlix MD 2.4 stars, and the best-rated incumbent's own users call its app something that needs a complete revamp; a Flutter app whose core promise is "never lose an entry, works without signal, stays logged in" attacks the exact failure mode.

## Implications for the first 90 days of product

1. Make data ownership a visible primitive. Local encrypted database on the phone, continuous local backup, one-tap export of everything (patients CSV, charts, invoices PDF, images zip), and a plain-language "your data is yours, even after you stop paying" page. This directly counters the Dentee/Practo/Eka export stories and the 47.5% data-loss fear.
2. Ship WhatsApp from the clinic's own number before anything fancy: appointment confirm/reminder, post-op instructions, invoice and prescription PDFs, recall nudges. Include a generous monthly quota in the base price; the per-pack resentment is loud. Treat a one-minute send lag as a bug.
3. Offline-first and boring reliability as the headline mobile promise: never lose a half-typed entry, no forced logouts, sync status visible, works in a basement clinic with no signal. These are the exact failures in BestoSys, Dentee, HealthPlix reviews.
4. Dental-only depth that Ray lacks: surface-level charting, phased treatment plans with per-visit cost, instalments and outstanding dues, estimates sendable from the phone, professional-looking receipts with signature, editable case numbers, allergy-aware prescribing.
5. Self-serve onboarding in under 10 minutes, no sales call, no document loops. Add a migration path from Practo Ray CSV export, Dentee, Excel and photographed paper case sheets.
6. Pricing: a solo plan at or under ₹999/month or ~₹10-12k/year, renewal price printed on the invoice, no per-booking or per-patient fees, no marketplace, no public listing without explicit consent. Say this out loud in marketing; it is the anti-Practo position dentists are already articulating.
7. Support as product: WhatsApp/phone support with a named person, published response window, in-app issue thread; the whole category loses customers on silence after payment.
8. GST done right in v1: per-line exempt/taxable tagging (treatment vs crowns/dentures/cosmetic), monthly GSTR-1-ready export, CA-friendly reports. Cheap to build, frequently requested, unhandled by incumbents.
9. Recall engine aimed at the money leak: incomplete-treatment list (RCT without crown, ortho missed visits), WhatsApp nudges, phased-cost conversation aids. Do not claim no-show reduction numbers you have not measured in your own pilots.
10. Front desk UI for an untrained assistant: minimal English, big buttons, few fields by default (name, phone, chief complaint), progressive disclosure for the dentist's clinical screens. Update off-hours; never surprise-redesign.
11. Keep lab tracking and consent forms simple in v1 (case status, due date, printable consent for wet signature then scan); verify the 3C register requirement with a CA before building anything.
12. Instrument your own baseline: patients/day, no-show rate, reminder response rate and treatment completion per pilot clinic, because no published Indian source has these numbers for solo practices.

## Open questions

- Patients per day, chairs per clinic and receptionist presence for solo and 1-3 chair clinics: no Indian survey measures these; needs 20-30 structured interviews or a pilot cohort.
- Adult, private-clinic no-show rate: the only measured figure is paediatric at a dental college (52% ever-missed); a 15-18% vendor number is unsourced.
- Is the "Practo sold our data" belief grounded in a specific incident (e.g., 2016-17 "technical glitches" plus Prime cross-marketing) or purely in marketplace behaviour? Worth asking ex-Ray dentists directly.
- Current Practo Ray dental share: "5000+ dentists" is vendor copy; unknown how many are active or paying.
- Annual vs monthly billing preference and tolerance for WhatsApp Business API pass-through costs at scale.
- Regional-language needs for staff UI and patient messages (Practo's higher plan is differentiated by local-language SMS).
- Whether ABDM/ABHA integration matters to solo dentists (vendors claim it; no dentist asked for it in any review read).
- r/IndianDentists and r/dentistry content, plus Facebook/Telegram dentist groups, remain unread because of access blocks; they may hold franker price and workflow discussion than Capterra.
- Applicability of the 3C register and of digitally signed consent for dental clinics (one dentist says mandatory; vendor says pre-software era).
- Lab turnaround and remake rates in India: no primary data found; only lab-side software reviews.
