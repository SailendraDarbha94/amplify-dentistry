## Compliance: India day one

Almost everything an Indian clinic app must do is already written down, but the regime with nine-figure penalties (DPDP) and the gate that decides whether you are listed as "ABDM-enabled" (the NHA sandbox exit) both land in 2027. Build the plumbing now, because clinics and auditors will ask for it at launch, and never let a certification gate block shipping the odontogram.

### Duties, translated into product

**DPDP consent and notice.** Section 6 consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous and withdrawable as easily as given, and s.5(3) obliges you to offer the notice in English or any of the 22 Eighth Schedule languages (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf). Rule 3 makes the notice standalone and itemised with links to withdraw and to complain to the Board (https://www.dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf). Health data is not a separate "sensitive" class under DPDP, but the penalty schedule is up to Rs 250 crore for security failures, Rs 200 crore for an unreported breach and Rs 200 crore for children's-data violations (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf). Model two unrelated consent objects: purpose-scoped data-processing consent (records, billing, WhatsApp reminders, marketing, photo use, third-party sharing) with notice version, language, channel, timestamp and withdrawal; and clinical procedure consent, which is a different legal act and does not substitute.

**Children.** Anyone under 18 is a child (s.2(f)); s.9 requires verifiable parental consent and bans tracking and targeted advertising. Rule 12 and the Fourth Schedule exempt "a clinical establishment ... or healthcare professional" from both duties only where processing is restricted to providing health services to the child (https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DPDP-Rules.pdf). "Healthcare professional" is defined through the NCAHP Act 2021, so a dentist's fit is uncertain; the safer hook is the clinic as a "clinical establishment", and recall promotions aimed at a minor fall outside the exemption. Store a verified guardian relationship and a minor flag that routes all messaging to the guardian until 18.

**Breach register with three clocks.** DPDP Rule 7: tell each affected patient "without delay", tell the Board without delay, and file the detailed report within 72 hours; no prescribed form exists (https://www.dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf). CERT-In: 6 hours (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf). An ABDM/NHA incident channel is asserted by vendors only [unverified].

**Retention and erasure.** The floors stack. DCI Code 3.3.1 sets a minimum of three years from commencement of treatment and 3.3.2 requires copies within 72 hours against receipt (https://dciindia.gov.in/Rule_Regulation/Gazette_Notification_reg_DCI_Revised_Dentists_Code_of_Ethics_Regulations_2014_27.06.2014.pdf). Income-tax Rule 6F wants a Form 3C daily case register kept six years, though its "medical" wording has not been formally held to cover dentists [unverified] (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/56057029/). The MoHFW EHR Standards 2016 say records "must compulsorily be preserved and not destroyed during the life-time of the person, ever" (https://www.nrces.in/download/files/pdf/nrces_ehr_stand_india.pdf). CPA 2019 s.69 gives complainants two years, condonable, and a minor's clock restarts at 18 (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/36183074/); the Sabka Dentist complaint ran from 2018 treatment to an August 2026 order (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/82459637/). DPDP s.8(7) requires erasure once purpose ends "unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law", and Rule 8(3) requires one year of processing logs (https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/0ecfe5fee4f08c90ee7a1dbb5b2e6b7c.pdf); the 48-hour pre-erasure notice in Rule 8(2) applies only to large e-commerce, gaming and social-media platforms, not clinics. Defaults: adults 10 years from last visit, minors to age 28, implants for the device lifetime, deceased three years then inactive; archive rather than hard-delete; honour DPDP erasure immediately for CRM data and refuse it for clinical records while a legal basis applies, logging the basis.

**Prescriptions.** Rule 2(ee)(iv) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules makes a registered dentist a "Registered medical practitioner", so Rule 65(10) applies: in writing, signed with the usual signature, dated, patient name and address, total quantity and dose, with single dispensing unless the prescriber states otherwise (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/acts_rules/2016DrugsandCosmeticsAct1940Rules1945.pdf). PCI Pharmacy Practice Regulations 2015 Reg 2(j) expressly accept "a written or electronic direction ... such as Dentist" and list what a pharmacist checks: qualification, registration number, age, sex, Rx symbol, signature (https://www.pci.nic.in/pdf/Pharmacy%20Practice%20Regulations.pdf). The Code adds the registration number on every prescription, certificate and receipt (3.4.1), full name, degrees and registration particulars on the letterhead (5.7.2), generic names "as far as possible" (3.5) and a flag when the dentist dispensed (5.7.1). A digital signature is recommended by the EHR Standards, not mandated by any rule [unverified as a matter of law]: ship a captured "usual signature" image plus a tamper-evident hash and verification QR, and offer Aadhaar eSign (IT Act s.3A) as an upgrade. Tag every drug with its Schedule and an effective date: H1 (retailer register kept three years) covers cefixime, ceftriaxone and other third- and fourth-generation cephalosporins, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin, tramadol, codeine and the benzodiazepines; pregabalin joins around 16 Nov 2026 under G.S.R. 377(E) (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadGazette_NotificationsFiles/2026.05.13%20G.S.R.%20377(E)_Notification%20for%20amendment%20in%20Schedule%20H1%20for%20inclusion%20of%20Pregabalin%20and%20its%20drug%20formulations.pdf), and draft G.S.R. 745(E) of 19 Aug 2026 proposes gabapentin, zopiclone, carisoprodol and flupentixol (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadGazette_NotificationsFiles/2026.08.19%20G.S.R%20745(E)_Inclusion%20of%20additional%20four%20drugs%20in%20Schedule%20H1.pdf). Amoxicillin, metronidazole, clindamycin, azithromycin, NSAIDs and chlorhexidine stay ordinary Schedule H. Block Schedule X and NDPS drugs from every remote flow.

**Teledentistry.** There is no safe harbour: the TPG defines an RMP under the IMC Act 1956 (https://www.nrces.in/download/files/pdf/Telemedicine.pdf), the NDC gazette list shows only the NDC Rules 2024 and accounts rules (https://dciindia.gov.in/GazetteList.aspx), and no dentist case involving WhatsApp or video since 2020 was found on Indian Kanoon [gap-fill-unverified]. Ship video as "tele-triage and follow-up", implement the TPG controls voluntarily (identity and registration number on screen and in every message, explicit consent record, interaction log, List O/A/B drug gating, H1 only as refills for existing in-person patients) and never market "TPG-compliant".

**Clinical consent is case law, not regulation.** The 2014 Code contains the word "consent" zero times; the standard is Samira Kohli (2008): real consent on nature, purpose, risks and alternatives, and a relative cannot consent for a competent adult (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/438423/). Fora awarded Rs 5 lakh where RCT consent was oral only (Sravani Dental, NCDRC 2022) (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/29669611/), Rs 3 lakh where a father never signed for a minor's orthodontic extraction (Prasenjit Das, WB SCDRC 2024) (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/85276897/), and Rs 4.32 lakh plus Rs 2 lakh where a chain could not produce pre-operative IOPAs for nine RCTs (Sabka Dentist, Maharashtra SCDRC, Aug 2026) (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/82459637/). Your {tooth_numbers} templates are the right shape; add the named operator, risks and alternatives, estimate reference, signer capacity, a free-text discussion note, OTP or stylus e-signature with hash and device metadata, and a guard that blocks "start RCT" without an attached radiograph or a documented reason.

**GST on dentistry.** Health-care services by a clinical establishment are exempt under entry 74 of Notification 12/2017 (SAC 999312); para 2(zg) excludes "cosmetic or plastic surgery" except reconstruction after congenital defects, developmental abnormalities, injury or trauma, and the 2022 Maharashtra AAR (Jyoti Ceramic) held crowns, bridges and restoratives exempt while bleaching and smile-design veneers were taxable under SAC 999722 at the then 18% (https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/no-gst-on-providing-dental-health-care-services-by-dental-clinic-aar/). Notification 15/2025-CT(R) moved group 99972 "beauty and physical well-being services" to a mandatory 5% without ITC from 22 Sep 2025 (https://www.gstindiaonline.com/pages/gstlegislation/GST-Nfy/Central%20Tax%20Rate/2025/15-2025-CTR.pdf); whether aesthetic dentistry sits in 99972 (5%, no ITC) or in 9993 outside the exemption (18%, ITC) is disputed between the checkers and unresolved by any CBIC circular or later AAR. HSN 9021 implants, artificial teeth and dental fittings are 5%, and 9018/9022 instruments and X-ray units fell from 12% to 5% on 22 Sep 2025 (https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/faq.pdf); a crown supplied inside treatment is a composite supply with exempt health care as the principal supply, while an over-the-counter retainer is a 5% goods sale. Exempt-only clinics need not register at all (CGST s.23); once any taxable line exists the threshold is Rs 20 lakh of aggregate turnover including exempt supplies, and whether Arunachal Pradesh is a Rs 10 lakh special-category state is disputed between checkers, so keep a state table. Rule 46A lets a registered clinic issue one "invoice-cum-bill of supply" to an unregistered patient covering exempt and taxable lines (https://taxhandout.com/gst-on-dentists-dental-clinics/), and e-invoicing applies only to B2B lines of taxpayers above Rs 5 crore (https://cleartax.in/s/e-invoicing-gst). Your own subscription is 18% with the place of supply at the dentist's address on record (https://cleartax.in/s/section-194o).

**Data localisation.** DPDP itself has only a negative list (s.16) with no country notified, Rule 15 conditions on foreign-state access and a Rule 13(4) power to localise Significant Data Fiduciaries' data (https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/). Host in India anyway: the approved ABDM Health Data Management Policy says "No personal data shall be stored beyond the geographical boundaries of India" (the 2022 revision reported at https://www.medianama.com/2022/06/223-new-health-data-management-policy/ was a draft) and NHA's integrator FAQ requires India-based callback servers with NHA NAT IPs whitelisted (https://sandboxcms.abdm.gov.in/uploads/FAQ_20_11_2025_808a25df64.pdf). Supabase ap-south-1 (Mumbai) does this on Pro at $25 a month (https://supabase.com/pricing); make region a tenant attribute from the first migration.

**ABDM for dentists.** Dentistry is a first-class system of medicine in the HPR with eleven dental specialities verified by the State Dental Council, and the HFR offers "Dental Clinic" with a "Number of Dental chairs" field and an optional Clinical Establishments Act ID (https://nhpr.abdm.gov.in/nhpr/v4/assets/index-7a48ab1a.js) [gap-fill-unverified]. The National Dental Register now runs at ndr.abdm.gov.in (NDR counter 405,961 in the same month as the 405,777 Part-A registered dentists (dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx, read Oct 2026) figure used throughout; https://dciindia.gov.in/), the clearest sign that the NMC pattern (HFR IDs within 7 days, HMIS integration within 15 days, non-compliant private colleges named on 17 Jul 2026) (https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/951) will reach dental colleges first and private clinics later. The exit path is M1 ABHA on v3 APIs only, M2 HIP linking with all 8 HI types including InvoiceRecord, M3 HIU, M4 native HPR/HFR, then functional testing by one of nine NHA-empanelled agencies within 7 working days, a CERT-In WASA done separately for web, Android and iOS and renewed annually, and two NHA demos (https://sandboxcms.abdm.gov.in/api/newmenus?populate=deep). The "any two milestones" shortcut repeated by vendors is not in official text; NHA's matrix requires M1+M2+M3+HFR for an HMIS listing [disputed]. DHIS money is trivial: Rs 5 per OPConsult, Prescription or Invoice record to the clinic and Rs 2.5 to you above 100 KYC-verified ABHA-linked records a month, and only once the software is integrated to M3 with a valid WASA (https://abdm.gov.in/strapicms/uploads/DHIS_corrigendum_7_c1d7e09cc2.pdf). Plan M1, M2 and M4 in the sandbox in the first 90 days, exit gates in days 90-150, M3 in months 4-6, with a Rs 3-10 lakh year-one certification reserve (NHA publishes no tariff) [unverified]. NRCeS IG v6.5.0 has no dental profile (https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html); encode tooth findings as Condition and Procedure with SNOMED tooth-structure bodySite inside OPConsultRecord and adopt the SNOMED CT Odontogram refset (July 2026 edition), free under the India affiliate licence (https://mlds.ihtsdotools.org/api/releasePackages/173263).

**Licences the clinic already juggles.** AERB registration of every X-ray unit through eLORA at no fee, QA every two years and a status update every six months (30,557 licensed dental units in 2024) (https://www.aerb.gov.in/images/PDF/Annual_report/ar2024/English/Annual_report-2024-eng.pdf); BMW Rules 2016 with a one-time Form II authorisation, a daily register, Form IV by 30 June and five-year retention, enforced through 14,783 show-cause notices in 2024 (https://cpcb.gov.in/uploads/Projects/Bio-Medical-Waste/AR_BMWM_2024.pdf); Clinical Establishments Act registration in Arunachal at Rs 1,000 with no dental minimum standards notified (https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-06/46311.pdf). NABH DHSP 3rd edition Section B (80 standards, 416 objective elements for 1-15 chairs) is the best checklist: entries signed, dated and timed within one hour, weekly biological sterilisation tests, monthly facility rounds, displayed tariff and written estimates (https://nabh-portal-live.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/13105241/Final-Dental-Standard-3rd-Edition.pdf).

**Payments and card data.** Never touch a PAN: RBI's card-on-file tokenisation framework (circular RBI/2021-22/96 of 7 Sep 2021, deadline extended to 1 Oct 2022 by RBI/2022-23/77; https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx) has applied since 1 Oct 2022. Never hold patient money: the Payment Aggregator Master Direction of 15 Sep 2025 needs Rs 15 crore net worth and requires merchant funds to land "in the merchant's bank account only" (https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/notification/PDFs/141MD7D7F25DEBF1F48449E20D685E4B014E5.PDF), so each clinic is a sub-merchant (Razorpay Route, or Cashfree Easy Split at 0.2% per split (https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-charges/)) and your margin is a programmatic split. UPI Collect was deprecated on 28 Feb 2026, so use Intent on Android and dynamic QR on web (https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/upi/); UPI P2M above Rs 2,000 carries 0.4% MDR capped at Rs 300 from 15 Oct 2026 unless NPCI defers to January 2027, with AutoPay debits exempt (https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-above-rs-2000-october-15/); e-mandates up to Rs 15,000 per debit run without additional authentication (https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13374).

**App stores.** Google Play's Health apps declaration has been mandatory since 31 Aug 2024 and Medical apps must ship from an Organization account with a D-U-N-S number, a public non-PDF privacy policy, a "not a medical device" disclaimer and no Play Billing for regulated clinical services (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en). Apple 5.1.3 bars personal health information in iCloud and 5.1.1(v) requires in-app account deletion (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

## Compliance: going global later

The Indian architecture carries abroad; the numbers get stricter and a few modules (claims, NHS banding, in-country hosting) are per-market add-ons. The one global line not to cross anywhere: anything that analyses a radiograph, CBCT or intraoral photo for pathology is a medical device. The FDA's final CDS guidance of 29 Jan 2026 says software that processes medical images fails criterion 1 and that recommendations aimed at patients rather than clinicians "meet the definition of a device" (https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); CDSCO's July 2026 software guidance says scheduling, billing and record systems are not devices but become one the moment they add image analysis or decision support (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/system/modules/CDSCO.WEB/elements/download_file_division.jsp?num_id=MTQ1MTM=). Partner with Pearl or Overjet later (at least 12 Overjet and 8 Pearl 510(k) clearances per openFDA, queried Oct 2026; neither lists a CDSCO licence) (https://api.fda.gov/device/510k.json?search=applicant:%22overjet%22&limit=20); keep voice notes, WhatsApp bots and plan explainers unregulated by making them restate the dentist's plan, never generate one.

Reasoning. ₹1,499 matches Practo Ray's unlimited-doctor tier while beating Clinicea's per-practitioner model for any two-dentist clinic. Against clinic economics it is invisible: Clove's clinics break even at about ₹4.75 lakh a month and mature at ₹10 lakh (https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/deep-dive/clove-dentals-prescription-for-scale/2993977/1), so ₹1,499 is 0.15-0.3% of revenue, and a first-year owner taking home ₹80,000-2 lakh a month (https://www.dentskart.com/blog/how-to-start-a-dental-practice-in-india-2026) pays about 1-2% of income. The free tier is the funnel: 54.5% of recent Kerala graduates earned ₹15,000 a month or less and only 12.2% of those working in dentistry fully owned a clinic (https://jglobaloralhealth.org/unemployment-and-vulnerable-financial-situation-among-recent-dental-graduates-of-kerala-india-results-from-a-cross-sectional-study/), so the associate's personal patient log converts when they open a clinic. Cost supports it: Meta charges ₹0.115 per utility message (https://amomic.in/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india), so 1,500 reminders cost about ₹200 incl. GST, and platform cost per clinic is about $1 a month at 100 clinics on Supabase Pro ($25) plus PowerSync Pro ($49) (https://supabase.com/pricing) (https://powersync.com/pricing). Infrastructure margin is therefore above 80%, but all-in margin is only 20-63% at 100 clinics once ₹3-10 lakh a year of ABDM certification is amortised (see the cost table in the architecture section), crossing 70% past roughly 300-500 clinics. Export pricing sits at Open Dental's $149 level with unlimited users, a quarter of CareStack's $829 floor. Publish the prices, bill month-to-month, charge no setup, per-booking or payment-percentage fees and give export free; this is the exact inverse of the Denticon fixed annual increase and NexHealth 90-day-notice patterns reviewers resent.

### Add-on monetisation

- WhatsApp marketing credits: Meta's ₹0.8631 plus 18% GST is about ₹1.02 (https://amomic.in/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india); retail at ₹1.50-2.00, below Fresha's ₹1.60-5.50 (https://www.fresha.com/pricing); bundle utility, meter marketing.
- Payments: one published payments rule: ₹0 on cash and static-QR UPI collected in clinic; a single printed 0.25% platform split, over the payment aggregator's negotiated rate, only on gateway-routed remote payments and EMI; UPI MDR passed through as its own line. Cashfree's rack rate is 1.95% with Easy Split at 0.2% (https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-charges/), so an ₹8,000 RCT paid remotely yields about ₹20.
- Membership plans: ₹499 a clinic a month, structured as prepaid preventive visits plus discounts and never "we pay if"; Clerri's US average is about $30 a month per member (https://clerri.com/platform-overview), and Clove sells ₹590-750 six-month plans (https://clovedental.in/membership/dental-health-plan-premium).
- AI voice notes: bundled in Clinic Plus as a fair-use allowance of 50 notes per dentist a month with metered overage (no separate add-on); store transcripts not audio; Kiroku charges £26.99-89.99 (https://www.trykiroku.com/).
- AI radiograph reads through a partner at ₹499-999 a month later; Open Dental sells the equivalent at $149 (https://www.opendental.com/site/fees.html).
- Lab marketplace: B2B 5% GST goods under HSN 9021 across roughly 2,403 directory-listed labs (https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-dental-laboratories-in-india); take rate 5-8%, year two.
- Patient financing: Bajaj via Razorpay costs the clinic 6-13.5% subvention by tenor (https://razorpay.com/pricing/); referral share needs term sheets, do not model as revenue yet.

## Go-to-market

### The wedge

Sell to the owner-dentist with one to three chairs, zero to seven years into ownership, in the five council areas that hold the most registered dentists: Karnataka (57,125), Maharashtra (55,672), Kerala (37,760), Tamil Nadu (36,824) and Andhra Pradesh (29,000), with Telangana for the founder's Telugu network (https://dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx). The all-India register shows 405,777 Part-A registered dentists (dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx, read Oct 2026), against 28,114 permitted BDS seats a year and 331 colleges (https://dciindia.gov.in/) (https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/dentistry-in-india-oversupply-and-gender-bias-shaping-oral-health-workforce). Chains are under 1.5% of clinics (Clove 715 clinics per Forbes India in May 2026, with Clove's own site showing 730+ as of Oct 2026; Apollo Dental 220+, Partha 120+, Sabka 100) (https://www.clovedental.in/) (https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/india-dental-services-market), they build in-house (Clove's CIO evaluated 10+ EMRs, found none "truly dental", and built a tap-first web EMR) (https://www.ciol.com/cxo-of-the-week-vikas-sood-cio-clove-dental/), and the Western incumbents are chasing 100-location DSOs, so nobody is designing for one dentist on one phone. The real competitor is paper, Excel and a personal WhatsApp number: 96.2% of Pune GDPs had WhatsApp, 72.3% took patient queries on it and 63.2% of those sharing images for second opinions obtained no consent (https://www.thejcdp.com/doi/10.5005/jp-journals-10024-2415); a Maharashtra survey of 265 dentists (PMC12375830, published 2025, fieldwork 2023) found 88.3% prefer WhatsApp for sharing patient data and only 46% always get consent (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12375830/). The demo that wins is ten seconds long: tap a tooth, mark the procedure done, the invoice appears, WhatsApp sends it. The software market itself is small (USD 88.8M in 2025 growing 13.4% a year to USD 241.9M by 2033 per Grand View) (https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/dental-practice-management-software-market/india), which is exactly why volume, low CAC and transactional add-ons matter more than category growth. Use Oasis Dental Clinic as the design partner for the hard cases: flaky connectivity, Clinical Establishments Act registration, a state with no CBWTF.

### First 100 clinics, month by month

The product spec's 90-day build puts the odontogram at item 4 and invoicing at item 9, and the founder is new to Flutter, so this calendar runs two months behind the first draft on purpose.

Targets at 100 paying clinics: Rs 1.5 lakh MRR at a blended Rs 1,500 ARPU, CAC under Rs 3,000 (two months of revenue), activation defined as the first invoice generated from a charted procedure and the first reminder delivered.

### Expansion sequencing

South and west India first, then north. Abroad, follow the dentists: Oman and the UAE first (Dentee already sells in Oman (https://www.dentee.com/); DHA licensing is an exam of 150 MCQs with no practical, so the Indian BDS pipeline is deep (https://academically.com/blogs/how-indian-dentists-can-work-in-dubai/); in-country hosting is required and Supabase has no Gulf region); the UK second (53.1% of dentists newly added to the GDC register in 2025 were non-UK qualified, 1,305 of 2,457, and the IDA itself sells an ORE pathway at Rs 4.82 lakh) (https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/05/07/international-dentists-joining-gdc-register-outnumber-uk-qualifiers-for-the-first-time/) (https://gdp.ida.org.in/), priced against Dentally's £125-945 per site with an NHS banding module; Australia third; the US last, because it needs a CDT licence, a BAA stack, a clearinghouse and cleared AI partners before the first sale.
