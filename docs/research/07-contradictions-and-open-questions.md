# Contradictions, uncertainties and open questions

Where sources disagreed, where fact-checkers corrected or disputed claims that matter, and the cross-section inconsistencies the editor found. The fixes below have been applied to the section documents; the strategic ones (open-sourcing the odontogram, the payments rule, the GTM calendar) are recorded as decisions in the [executive summary](00-executive-summary.md).

> Part of the [dental practice-management research](README.md) (October 2026).

## Contradictions and uncertainties

- Dentist headcount: 405,777 Part-A dentists (DentistRegistered.aspx, market section) vs 405,961 (dciindia.gov.in homepage/NDR, compliance section) vs '~405,800'; the compliance section uses both figures. Different snapshot dates, not a real conflict, but one number must be chosen and date-stamped.
- Clove Dental size: 715 clinics in May 2026 (Forbes India, market section) vs '730+' (clovedental.in, GTM section). Both can be true; date-stamp.
- NDC/DCI transition: the market section tags the 19 Mar 2026 dissolution [confirmed] and mandates 'NDC / State Dental Council' on every form, while the compliance section shows the NDC still processing registrations under s.34(2)(b) of the Dentists Act on 30 Sep 2026 and tags commencement [disputed between two checkers]. Keep regulator names, codes and retention rules data-driven.
- Cosmetic-dentistry GST: 5% without ITC (group 99972 since 22 Sep 2025) vs 18% with ITC is unresolved by any CBIC circular or later AAR; the product spec defaults to 18%, the market section leaves it open; all sections agree it must be a configurable per-clinic rate with a rate-history table.
- GST registration threshold for Arunachal Pradesh: ₹10 lakh (one checker) vs ₹20 lakh (two checkers). Editor's note, unverified: post-2019 only Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura appear to retain the ₹10 lakh services threshold, so ₹20 lakh is the likelier default; confirm with the CA and keep the state table.
- UPI MDR (0.4% capped at ₹300 above ₹2,000 from 15 Oct 2026): five days away at the time of writing, deferral to Jan 2027 sought, Supreme Court notices issued 29 Sep 2026; the architecture section alone adds an exemption for merchants under ₹1 lakh a month, which would exempt many year-one ICP clinics. All gap-fill-unverified, and the same medianama article is cited under two different slugs.
- WhatsApp in-window service replies after 1 Oct 2026: BSPs say ₹0.115 beyond 1,000 free per number per month; Meta's own page still shows them free [disputed]. The date has passed, so this is now checkable; budget as if the BSPs are right.
- Meta's AI-provider fee: the product spec says it applies only to general-purpose assistants; the architecture section says it applies only in Brazil; both cite the same llms.txt. Either reading keeps a clinic booking bot unaffected, but the sentence should be reconciled.
- Gross margin: the pricing section claims 'above 80%' at 100 clinics counting only Supabase and PowerSync; the architecture section's all-in COGS at 100 clinics is ₹560–1,185 per clinic (ABDM amortisation and WhatsApp included), i.e. 20–63% on ₹1,499, crossing 70–80% only past a few hundred clinics.
- WhatsApp bundle: market advantage 3 promises 'unlimited utility messages'; the pricing plans cap Solo/Clinic/Plus at 100/1,500/5,000; the architecture cost model assumes 1,800 per clinic (600 visits a month), which exceeds the Clinic quota and the ICP's 12–18 patients a day (about 300–450 visits).
- Payments take: the product spec (principle 7) and market section promise 'no percentage of collections' and 'decline to mark up'; the pricing section plans a 0.2–0.3% platform split and the architecture section 0.2–0.5%. Needs one published rule: zero on in-clinic cash and static-QR UPI, a printed split only on gateway-routed remote payments and EMI.
- Open-sourcing the odontogram (product spec principle 10, v3 roadmap, architecture 'best path') contradicts the market section's claim that the FDI surface-level odontogram is 'the thing nobody can copy'. Strategic decision, not a wording fix.
- ABDM timeline: product spec v2 places M1/M2/M4 in months 4–9; the market, architecture and compliance sections place sandbox M1/M2/M4 inside the first 90 days, exit gates in days 90–150 and M3 in months 4–6; the GTM calendar lists the ABDM-enabled listing in months 10–12. Also [disputed]: the vendor 'any two milestones' shortcut vs NHA's M1+M2+M3+HFR matrix for an HMIS listing; confirm with integration.support@nha.gov.in.
- DHIS incentive (₹5 per record to the clinic, ₹2.5 to the vendor above 100 KYC-linked records a month): Corrigendum 7 was valid only to Sep 2026, so the scheme may have lapsed or been re-issued; treat as marketing, never revenue.
- GTM calendar vs MVP: GTM Month 1 (Oct 2026, three weeks left) ships schedule, odontogram, consent, invoice and WhatsApp and Month 2 runs a five-clinic beta, while the product spec's 90-day build puts the odontogram at item 4 and invoicing at item 9, with a founder new to Flutter. The calendar is roughly two months optimistic.
- Front-desk surface: the product spec excludes the Flutter-web front desk for 90 days (v2); the architecture's reference architecture names app_desk (Flutter web plus Windows) as a day-one target, yet its own comparison table gives web to React/Next.js and the market section's technical risk says to spike Flutter web before choosing it over Next.js. Unresolved.
- Staff login OTP: the product spec MVP uses MSG91 SMS OTP (₹0.18–0.25); the architecture section defaults to WhatsApp authentication templates (₹0.115) with SMS fallback. Both still require DLT registration for the fallback.
- Evidence age and provenance: the Pune WhatsApp survey (JCDP 10024-2415; 96.2% / 63.2%) appears to be a 2018–19 paper; the Kerala graduate income study is pre-2019 and single-state; the Maharashtra 265-dentist survey is dated '2023' in the market section and '2025' in the GTM section; '31 features, I use 4' and Dento365's '68% on paper' are uncited vendor lines; G2/Capterra/Software Advice returned HTTP 403 so the complaint columns relay RevUp Dental's 2026 roundup.
- Card-on-file circular: product spec and architecture cite RBI NotificationUser Id=12159; the compliance section cites Id=12345. One is wrong (or they are the Sept 2021 tokenisation circular and the June 2022 extension); verify and use one.
- FDA clearances: 'at least 12 Overjet and 8 Pearl' (product spec, openFDA query) vs 'Overjet holds 10+' (compliance, Overjet's own blog). Use the openFDA count with a query date.
- SDCEP MRONJ incidence figures (<0.05% osteoporosis-level, <5% cancer-level) are used in the product spec's medical-history spec but tagged [disputed] in the market section; only the structure (record a risk group, 8-week socket review) is confirmed. SDCEP is Scottish guidance with no NDC equivalent.
- Gap-fill-unverified blocks that the sections lean on heavily: consumer-fora awards (₹5 lakh Sravani Dental, ₹3 lakh Prasenjit Das, ₹4.32 lakh plus ₹2 lakh Sabka Dentist), CPCB 14,783 show-cause notices, AERB 30,557 licensed units, NABH DHSP 80 standards/416 elements, HPR/HFR dental fields read from a JavaScript bundle, WASA/functional-testing costs of ₹3–10 lakh, RBI PA Master Direction thresholds, the 'eSushrut Clinic' HMIS, and the SNOMED India affiliate-licence terms.
- Hiring-pool data (about 1,068 Flutter vs 6,413 React Native postings) is a mid-2025 US LinkedIn snapshot tagged [disputed], with no Indian split; the only evidence against React Native Skia for canvas work is a single un-benchmarked blog post, also [disputed].
- Un-tagged recency claims that the stack decision rests on: Flutter 3.47 released 12 Aug 2026, package:material_ui 1.0 shipping with it, Expo SDK 57 on 30 Jun 2026, a React Native Skia 'Graphite' tag on 2 Oct 2026. Verify each before pinning versions.
- ABDM Health Data Management Policy: the architecture section cites a 2022 draft for the 'no personal data beyond India' clause; the compliance section treats it as policy in force. India-only hosting is a practical must either way because NHA requires India-hosted callback servers (itself gap-fill-unverified).
- Retention: MoHFW EHR Standards 2016 say records must be preserved for the patient's lifetime 'ever', while the architecture and compliance defaults are 10 years from last visit (adults), age 28 (minors) and device lifetime (implants); reconciled only by 'inactive, never deleted', and the Standards are recommendatory [disputed].
- Clinic-count denominators are unsourced: '95% individual practitioners' is a 2011-era estimate, Dentalkart claims 2–2.5 lakh clinics, chains are 'under 1.5% of clinics' by count but 6.6% of a USD 4.3bn services market by value (Ken Research). Every TAM statement inherits this uncertainty.
- Google Play Health-apps declaration effective date (31 Aug 2024) is [disputed on dates] in two sections; the requirements themselves (Organization account with D-U-N-S, non-PDF privacy policy, not-a-device disclaimer, no Play Billing for clinical services) are confirmed.

## Cross-section fixes (applied)

- **compliance-and-gtm (GTM wedge, KEY NUMBERS)**: Cites 405,961 dentists (dciindia.gov.in homepage) and 405,777 Part-A dentists (DentistRegistered.aspx) in the same section; the market section uses ~405,800 and 405,777.
  - FIX: Use '405,777 Part-A registered dentists (dciindia.gov.in/DentistRegistered.aspx, read Oct 2026)' in all four sections; if the NDR counter is kept, label it 'NDR counter, 405,961, same month'.
- **compliance-and-gtm (GTM wedge)**: States 'Clove 730+' while the market section says 715 clinics in May 2026 (Forbes India).
  - FIX: Write '715 clinics (Forbes India, May 2026; Clove's own site shows 730+ as of Oct 2026)' and use the same phrasing in both.
- **compliance-and-gtm (GTM wedge)**: Describes the 265-dentist Maharashtra WhatsApp survey as '2025'; the market section calls it a '2023' survey (same PMC12375830 source).
  - FIX: Verify the publication year once and use 'PMC12375830 (published 2025, fieldwork 2023)' or the corrected single year in both sections.
- **market-and-positioning (Risks: Market, TAM bullet)**: ARR arithmetic uses ₹1,000 per clinic ('10,000 clinics ... about INR 12 crore ARR') while the pricing section lists ₹1,499 and the GTM targets a ₹1,500 blended ARPU.
  - FIX: Change to '10,000 clinics at a ₹1,500 blended ARPU is about ₹18 crore ARR (₹12 crore at ₹1,000)'.
- **compliance-and-gtm (Pricing: Reasoning)**: Claims gross margin 'stays above 80%' at 100 clinics by counting only Supabase ($25) and PowerSync ($49); the architecture cost table puts all-in COGS at ₹560–1,185 per clinic at 100 clinics once ABDM certification and WhatsApp are included.
  - FIX: Rewrite as 'infrastructure margin above 80%; all-in margin 20–63% at 100 clinics because ₹3–10 lakh a year of ABDM certification is amortised, crossing 70% past roughly 300–500 clinics' and cite the architecture table.
- **market-and-positioning (Unfair advantage 4)**: Recommends bundling 'unlimited utility messages', while the pricing plans cap Solo/Clinic/Plus at 100/1,500/5,000 and the architecture cost model assumes 1,800 per clinic.
  - FIX: Replace 'unlimited' with 'fair-use quota: 1,500 utility messages in Clinic (about 500 visits at 3 touches), 5,000 in Plus, overage at Meta cost plus GST' and use that wording in the pricing section.
- **architecture-and-data (Costs at 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 clinics, assumptions)**: Assumes 600 visits and 1,800 utility messages per clinic per month (about 24 patients a day), but the market section's ICP reaches 12–18 patients a day by month 12 (about 300–450 visits).
  - FIX: Model a base case of 400 visits / 1,200 messages (about ₹165 incl. GST per clinic) and keep 600 / 1,800 (about ₹245) as the mature-clinic case; carry both into the pricing section's COGS sentence.
- **compliance-and-gtm (Pricing: Add-on monetisation, Payments) and architecture-and-data (Integrations: Payments)**: A 0.2–0.3% (pricing) or 0.2–0.5% (architecture) platform split on collections contradicts product-spec principle 7 and the market section's 'no 2% on your collections / decline to mark up' positioning.
  - FIX: Publish one rule in all sections: ₹0 on cash and static-QR UPI collected in clinic; a single printed 0.25% platform split, over the PA's negotiated rate, only on gateway-routed remote payments and EMI, with UPI MDR passed through as its own line.
- **product-spec (Principle 10, Roadmap v3) and architecture-and-data (best path under Flutter)**: Both recommend open-sourcing the odontogram; the market section calls the FDI surface-level odontogram 'the thing nobody can copy from a package', i.e. the moat.
  - FIX: Decide now and state it once: either keep chart_engine proprietary, or open-source only the tooth geometry and FDI/Universal/Palmer notation layer and keep surfaces, conditions, perio, history and sync proprietary.
- **product-spec (Roadmap v2 row)**: Places ABDM M1, M2 and M4 in months 4–9; the market, architecture and compliance sections put sandbox M1/M2/M4 in the first 90 days, exit gates in days 90–150 and M3 in months 4–6; the GTM calendar puts the ABDM-enabled listing in months 10–12.
  - FIX: Rewrite the v2 row as 'sandbox M1/M2/M4 done in days 0–90 (engineering only, no customer-facing dependency); functional testing and three WASA certificates days 90–150; M3 months 4–6; ABDM-enabled listing month 7–9 at the earliest, months 10–12 as the buffered GTM date'.

## Open questions still worth closing before launch

- Patients per day, chairs, and receptionist presence for solo and 1-3 chair Indian clinics: no survey measures these; 20-30 structured interviews or the Oasis pilot cohort must supply them.
- Adult private-clinic no-show rate in India: only a paediatric figure (52% ever-missed) exists; vendor figures (15-18%, "70% reduction") are unsourced.
- Whether the "Practo sold our data" belief is grounded in an incident or in marketplace behaviour; ask ex-Ray dentists directly.
- Cosmetic-dentistry GST: 5% without ITC (group 99972) vs 18%; and whether Arunachal Pradesh keeps a Rs 10 lakh threshold; both need a CA opinion.
- ABDM sandbox exit gates (vendor "any two milestones" vs NHA's M1+M2+M3+HFR matrix) and real WASA and functional-testing costs; confirm with integration.support@nha.gov.in.
- UPI MDR of 0.4% above Rs 2,000 from 15 Oct 2026: deferral and Supreme Court notices were pending at the time of writing.
- WhatsApp in-window service replies after 1 Oct 2026: BSPs say Rs 0.115 beyond 1,000 free per number per month; Meta's page still showed them free.
- Default export folders, filename patterns and auto-export options of Woodpecker Ai-Dental, Vatech EzDent-i and Xpect Vision software on Windows 10/11 (hands-on testing with each sensor).
- Whether a stylus e-signature on consent is accepted by Indian consumer fora in practice; one dentist on Quora says paper only, the law (IT Act s.10A) says otherwise; get a legal opinion before marketing "legally valid e-signatures".
- The 3C daily case register (Income-tax Rule 6F) applicability to dentists.
- r/IndianDentists and Facebook/Telegram dentist groups were unreadable by the research tools; they may hold franker price and workflow discussion.
